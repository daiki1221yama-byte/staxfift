## The real pain: hiring without a recruiting team

If you're an HR manager wearing five hats, a founder hiring your tenth employee, or an office manager who suddenly "owns recruiting," the pain usually looks something like this:

- **Job posts are scattered.** You post on one job board, forget another, and lose track of where your best applicants came from.
- **Resumes live everywhere.** Some arrive by email, some through a job board, some as a referral via chat. Nobody has the full picture.
- **Feedback disappears.** The hiring manager liked a candidate, but said so in a hallway conversation. Two weeks later, nobody remembers why.
- **Scheduling eats your week.** Finding a time that works for a candidate and three interviewers can take more emails than the interview itself.
- **Candidates get ghosted.** Not on purpose — you just ran out of time to send rejection or update emails. That quietly hurts your employer brand.

None of these problems is dramatic on its own. Together, they slow hiring down, frustrate hiring managers, and cost you strong candidates who accepted another offer while you were still chasing feedback.

A good applicant tracking system (ATS) gives you one place for job posts, applicants, notes, interviews, and communication. The trick is picking one that matches the size and rhythm of your hiring, rather than one built for a 50-person recruiting department.

## How to judge an applicant tracking system for small business: 5 criteria

Here are the five things I'd look at first. They don't depend on any specific feature launch, so they stay useful no matter how the product pages change.

### 1. How the pricing model fits your hiring pattern

ATS pricing comes in a few shapes:

- **Flat tiers** — you pay a set monthly or annual amount for a plan, often with limits on features rather than seats.
- **Per recruiter or per user** — cost grows with the number of people who log in.
- **By company size** — cost grows with your total headcount, even if you hire rarely.
- **Quote-based** — you talk to sales and get a custom price.

If you have many hiring managers who need to leave feedback, per-user pricing can add up quickly. If you hire in bursts, a plan you can scale up and down may matter more than the sticker price.

### 2. Ease of use for occasional users

Your recruiter or HR lead will live in the tool, but hiring managers might log in twice a month. If they can't figure out how to leave a scorecard in under a minute, they'll go back to email. Look for a simple pipeline view, clean candidate profiles, and mobile-friendly feedback.

### 3. Job distribution and career site

Can you post to multiple job boards from one place? Do you get a branded careers page without needing a web developer? For small companies without a large recruiting budget, reaching applicants through free boards and a decent careers page is a big deal.

### 4. Automation and scheduling

Small teams benefit most from automation because they have the least time. Useful examples include automatic stage-based emails, interview self-scheduling, questionnaires that screen for must-have requirements, and reminders for interviewers who haven't left feedback.

### 5. Growth path: offers, onboarding, and HRIS connections

Think about what happens after a candidate says yes. Can you send offer letters and collect e-signatures? Does it connect to your HR system or payroll? You may not need these on day one, but switching ATS platforms later is painful, so check the path ahead.

### Breezy HR, in plain English

Breezy HR is an applicant tracking system designed around a drag-and-drop, visual hiring pipeline. Each job has stages — applied, phone screen, interview, offer, and so on — and you move candidates across them like cards on a board. That visual approach is the reason many small teams find it easy to adopt: hiring managers can see at a glance where every candidate stands.

Based on the official feature and pricing pages, here's what stands out:

- **Unlimited users and positions on every plan.** This is the headline. Instead of paying per hiring manager, you choose a plan by the features you need. For a company where many people take part in interviews, that is a meaningful difference.
- **Job distribution to 50+ job boards** from one place, plus a branded careers site.
- **Interview self-scheduling**, so candidates can pick a time that works instead of trading emails.
- **A customizable pipeline** with stage-based automation, so you can send emails or questionnaires when a candidate moves forward.
- **Electronic signatures** on mid-level plans, and offer management and HRIS integrations on higher plans.
- **Breezy Intelligence**, an AI add-on that helps score and summarize applicants. It runs on credits; the free trial includes a credit allowance.

Breezy offers a 14-day, full-feature free trial with no credit card required, which is enough time to set up a real job and see how your hiring managers react.

**The minor negative point:** Breezy doesn't have a free plan. If you hire only one or two people a year, a flat monthly subscription may be hard to justify, and a free or pay-as-you-go option could suit you better. Also, some of the features that growing teams care about most — e-signatures, offer management, and HRIS integrations — sit on the higher tiers, so check which plan you'll actually need before comparing prices.

### Workable

Workable is a well-established ATS with a strong reputation for job distribution. Its pricing page highlights posting to 200+ job boards, a careers page builder, self-scheduled interviews, native e-signatures, resume parsing, and offer letter templates. Workable has also leaned heavily into AI, with an agent that can help with sourcing and screening; AI usage is counted in credits.

Pricing is based on company-size bands rather than seats, and recruiting is bundled with HR features. There's a 15-day free trial with no credit card. Workable is a good choice if you want broad reach and are comfortable with pricing that scales with your headcount.

### JazzHR

JazzHR targets small and midsize businesses with a straightforward ATS. Its tiers cover posting to free job boards, knockout questions to filter applicants, AI-assisted candidate matching, structured interviews, and task automation on higher plans. Offers and e-signatures appear on the top tier.

Note that JazzHR's standard pricing is aimed at organizations up to a certain size, and its pricing page focuses on "Get started" and demo requests rather than advertising a free trial. It's worth a look if you want a no-frills tool and plan to grow into more features over time.

### Tellent Recruitee

Recruitee (now part of Tellent) is known for collaborative hiring. Its plans include multi-board posting, a careers site builder, pipeline automation, candidate self-scheduling, and, on higher tiers, offer letter generation, employee referrals, and an AI screening assistant. An HR system is available as an add-on.

Pricing depends on plan and employee count, and the official page mentions an annual contract minimum (with monthly payment possible). There's a free trial with no credit card required. Recruitee suits teams where many people collaborate on each hire.

### Greenhouse

Greenhouse is a heavyweight in structured hiring. It's built around interview kits, scorecards, and consistent evaluation, with strong reporting, sourcing and CRM tools, scheduling, and an AI notetaker. Pricing is quote-based, and the official page offers demos rather than a self-serve trial.

For a 15-person company hiring a few roles a year, Greenhouse is often more than you need. For a fast-scaling company that wants rigorous, consistent interviews across many teams, it's a serious contender.

### Lever

Lever combines an ATS with a recruiting CRM, so you can nurture passive candidates as well as manage applicants. It includes out-of-the-box reporting, AI-assisted screening and matching, interview transcripts and summaries, and fraud prevention signals. Like Greenhouse, pricing is quote-based and the path to trying it is a demo.

Lever shines for teams that do proactive sourcing and want to build talent pools over time. If most of your hires come from inbound applications, its CRM strengths may go underused.

### BambooHR

BambooHR is primarily an HR information system — employee records, time off, reporting, and more — with hiring built into the platform. Its core package includes job posting, candidate records, and email and offer letter templates, with limits on the number of open jobs that rise on higher tiers.

Pricing is per employee for companies above a certain size, with a flat rate for very small teams, and you request a quote rather than starting a self-serve trial. BambooHR makes sense if your main need is an HR system and you want "good enough" hiring inside it. If recruiting is your biggest headache, a dedicated ATS will usually go deeper.

### Zoho Recruit

Zoho Recruit is one of the few tools here with a genuine free plan, though it's limited to one active job per recruiter license. Paid plans add candidate sourcing, premium job boards, a branded careers site, employee referrals, assessments, and integrations, with AI matching and recruitment automation at the top tier. There's also a 15-day free trial of the top tier's features.

Pricing is per recruiter license, which works well for very small teams. It's especially attractive if you already use other Zoho apps. The interface can feel busier than Breezy's, so test it with a hiring manager who isn't technical.

## Breezy HR vs Workable: the most common head-to-head

Many small companies narrow their choice down to Breezy HR and Workable, so it's worth comparing them more closely.

**Pricing logic.** Breezy prices by plan features and includes unlimited users and positions. Workable prices by company-size band and bundles HR features with recruiting. If you're a small company that interviews with many people, Breezy's model can be easier to predict. If you're already looking for basic HR features too, Workable's bundle may be good value.

**Reach.** Workable advertises distribution to 200+ job boards; Breezy advertises 50+. For most small businesses, the big free boards matter most, but if you hire in niche markets or many regions, test how each tool handles the boards you care about.

**Ease of use.** Both are designed for non-specialists. Breezy's drag-and-drop pipeline is especially intuitive for hiring managers who log in occasionally. Workable's interface is clean as well, with more emphasis on AI-assisted sourcing.

**AI features.** Both offer AI add-ons that run on credits. If AI screening is a big part of your plan, compare how many credits come with each plan and what happens when you run out.

**The rule of thumb:** if collaboration and predictable pricing matter most, start with Breezy. If you want recruiting plus light HR in one package and broad distribution, start with Workable.

## How to run a smart free trial (so you actually learn something)

A free trial is only useful if you test the things that matter. Here's a simple plan you can follow in about a week.

1. **Set up one real job.** Don't use a dummy role. Write a real description, set up a real pipeline, and publish to the job boards you'd actually use.
2. **Customize your stages.** Add the steps you really follow — phone screen, skills task, team interview, reference check, offer. Note how easy it is to change them.
3. **Add a screening questionnaire.** Include two or three must-have questions (work authorization, location, a key skill). Check whether answers are easy to filter.
4. **Invite a hiring manager.** Ask them to review three candidates and leave feedback — without your help. If they get stuck, that's important information.
5. **Test interview scheduling.** Send a self-scheduling link to a colleague acting as a candidate. Check the calendar integration and the candidate's experience on a phone.
6. **Send stage-based emails.** Set up an automatic acknowledgment and a polite rejection template. Your candidates will notice.
7. **Look at reporting.** Can you tell where applicants came from and how long each stage takes? Those two numbers drive most improvements.

By the end of the week, you'll know more than any comparison article — including this one — can tell you.

## 5 mistakes small companies make when choosing an ATS

**1. Buying for the company you hope to be in five years.** Enterprise tools are powerful, but their setup and cost can overwhelm a small team. Choose for the next 12–24 months, with a reasonable growth path.

**2. Ignoring hiring managers.** The best ATS in the world fails if hiring managers won't use it. Include at least one in your trial.

**3. Comparing monthly prices only.** Look at what's included. A cheaper plan that charges per user or lacks scheduling may cost more in time and add-ons.

**4. Skipping the candidate experience.** Apply to your own job on a phone. If it's clunky, strong candidates will drop off before you ever see them.

**5. Forgetting about data.** Think about how you'll export candidate data if you switch later, and how the tool handles privacy requests. Ask before you commit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do small businesses really need an applicant tracking system?**
Not always. If you hire one person every year or two, a well-organized spreadsheet may be enough. But once you're hiring several roles a year, involving multiple interviewers, or receiving lots of applicants per role, an ATS usually pays for itself in time saved and candidates not lost.

**Is there a free version of Breezy HR?**
According to Breezy's official pricing page, there is no free plan. There is a 14-day free trial with full features and no credit card required, so you can test it properly before paying.

**Which ATS is easiest for non-HR people?**
Tools with a visual, drag-and-drop pipeline — like Breezy HR — tend to be easiest for occasional users. That said, ease of use is personal. The best test is to have a non-HR colleague try it during the trial.

**Can I move my candidate data if I switch tools later?**
Most ATS platforms offer some form of export, but notes, scorecards, and email history don't always transfer cleanly. Ask each vendor about export formats before you sign up.

**Do I need AI features?**
AI screening and summaries can save time when you get a high volume of applicants, but they're not essential for every team. Treat them as a helpful extra, review AI suggestions with human judgment, and check how usage is billed.

**How long does it take to set up an ATS?**
For a small company, a basic setup — careers page, one or two jobs, pipeline stages, and email templates — can often be done in a day or two. Integrations with HR systems and calendars may take longer.
