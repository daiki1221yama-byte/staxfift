## The real pain: selling courses as a small team

If you're a solo creator, a coach, or a small training business with a handful of staff, the challenges are surprisingly consistent:

- **You need it to look like *your* brand.** Corporate clients and serious learners judge your course by its presentation. A cluttered marketplace page doesn't help.
- **Your offers keep evolving.** You start with one course, then add a bundle, a membership, a cohort, a certificate program. The platform has to grow with you.
- **Fees add up quietly.** Subscription fees, transaction fees, payment processing, add-ons — it's easy to underestimate the real cost.
- **Learners need structure.** Quizzes, assignments, certificates, and progress tracking matter, especially if you train professionals or teams.
- **You don't have a tech team.** Every hour spent fixing integrations is an hour not spent teaching or selling.

The right course platform makes the business side quiet, so you can focus on content and learners. The wrong one gives you a monthly reminder of everything that doesn't connect.

## How to compare course platforms: 5 criteria that last

These criteria don't depend on any specific feature launch, so they stay useful as platforms evolve.

### 1. Course building and learning experience

How easy is it to create lessons, rearrange modules, and add quizzes, assignments, and certificates? Then switch sides: how does it feel to be a learner, especially on a phone?

### 2. Selling tools

Look for flexible pricing options — one-time payments, payment plans, subscriptions, bundles, coupons, upsells — and a smooth checkout. For training businesses, the ability to sell to groups or teams can also matter.

### 3. Total cost, including fees

Most platforms charge a subscription, and some also take a transaction fee on lower plans. Payment processing fees apply on top. Calculate your total cost at your expected revenue, not just the monthly price.

### 4. Marketing and community

Do you get landing pages, a website, and email tools? Is there a community area for learners? Built-in tools mean fewer integrations; separate tools mean more flexibility.

### 5. Room to grow

Check what higher plans unlock: more admin seats, advanced assessments, learning paths, SCORM support, single sign-on, branded mobile apps, and dedicated support. You don't want to switch platforms just as your business takes off.

### Thinkific, in plain English

Thinkific is a course platform built for people who care about teaching well. Its drag-and-drop course builder makes it easy to organize modules and lessons, and learning tools like quizzes, assignments, and completion certificates are available on every plan. Around that core, Thinkific adds communities, memberships, and a site builder so you can sell under your own brand and domain.

Here's what stands out from Thinkific's official pricing and feature pages:

- **Structured learning tools on all plans** — quizzes, assignments, and certificates, which matter a lot if you train professionals.
- **Communities** included, with the number of communities and spaces growing as you move up the plans.
- **Flexible selling** — memberships, subscriptions, bundles, upsells, and payment plans through Thinkific's checkout.
- **Thinkific Payments**, which the FAQ says avoids a third-party gateway fee (standard processing fees still apply).
- **Your own site** — landing pages, a site builder, and a custom domain.
- **AI tools**, including an AI course outline generator to help you get started.
- **Thinkific Plus** for larger training businesses, adding features like SSO, SCORM support, learning paths, exams and credentialing, and dedicated support.

There's no free plan, but the 30-day free trial is longer than many competitors offer, which gives you time to build a real course before paying.

**The minor negative point:** a branded mobile app (your own name and icon in the app stores) is a paid add-on rather than standard, and it's only included on select Plus plans. Standard plans also have caps on the number of current students, so very large audiences may need to plan for a higher tier. And while Thinkific includes email automation, creators who want deep marketing funnels sometimes prefer a more marketing-focused platform like Kajabi.

### Teachable

Teachable is one of the most familiar names in online courses. It focuses on making course and coaching sales simple, with drip content, quizzes, short-answer assessments, memberships, bundles, coupons, upsells, order bumps, and abandoned cart emails. It also includes built-in email marketing, an affiliate program, and student apps for iOS and Android. Certificates are listed on higher plans.

The main pricing detail to understand: the entry plan carries a transaction fee, while higher plans show no platform transaction fee when you use Teachable Payments (card processing still applies). There's no free plan; there is a free trial plus a money-back guarantee, and trial terms are shown on the pricing page.

### Kajabi

Kajabi aims to run your entire knowledge business in one place: website, landing pages, funnels, email, courses, coaching, memberships, and community. It's the most marketing-heavy option here, with unlimited email sends and contact limits that rise by plan. Kajabi states it takes no revenue share, but card processing fees apply, and an extra fee applies if you use a third-party processor on lower plans. There's a 14-day free trial.

Kajabi fits creators who want fewer tools and are comfortable with premium pricing. If your focus is structured learning rather than marketing, Thinkific may feel more natural.

### Podia

Podia is a friendly, simple platform for selling courses, digital downloads, coaching, events, and community, with a website, blog, and email included. The entry plan has a transaction fee; higher plans don't charge Podia fees (payment processor fees still apply). There's a 14-day free trial with no credit card required.

Podia is great for creators who sell a mix of small products and want a simple storefront. It's lighter on advanced learning features than Thinkific or LearnWorlds.

### LearnWorlds

LearnWorlds focuses on interactive learning: an AI course builder, assessments, SCORM support, certifications, learning paths, and a built-in community. Higher plans add white-label options, and a branded mobile app is available as an add-on. The entry plan charges a per-enrollment fee, while higher plans don't. There's a 30-day free trial.

LearnWorlds is a strong choice for training companies and educators who need detailed assessments and certifications. Some creators find it more complex to set up than Thinkific.

### Circle

Circle is a community platform first, with courses, live events, and payments built in. If your learners value peer discussion and live sessions as much as video lessons, Circle can be a great home. It charges transaction fees that decrease on higher tiers, and there's a 14-day free trial with no credit card. For deeply structured courses with assessments, a course-first platform usually goes further.

### Skool

Skool combines a simple community, courses, and a calendar in a uniform, easy-to-learn format, with a points-and-levels system to encourage participation. It has two flat tiers, and the transaction fee is the main difference between them. Skool is fast to launch, but it offers little in the way of branding, assessments, or certificates.

## Thinkific vs Teachable: the detailed head-to-head

This is the comparison most course creators care about, so let's go deeper.

**Course building.** Both offer easy builders. Thinkific is known for flexibility and learning depth — quizzes, assignments, and certificates are included on every plan. Teachable covers quizzes and assessments too, but places certificates on higher plans.

**Fees.** This is a key difference. Teachable's entry plan includes a transaction fee, which can matter as sales grow. Thinkific's pricing page doesn't list a platform transaction fee; with Thinkific Payments, you pay standard processing without a third-party gateway fee. Run both through your expected revenue before deciding.

**Marketing.** Teachable includes built-in email marketing and an affiliate program, which is handy for creators who want everything in one place. Thinkific includes email automation and a site builder; some creators still connect a dedicated email tool for advanced campaigns.

**Community.** Thinkific includes communities with spaces and live events, growing by plan. Teachable is more focused on courses and coaching, so creators who want an active learner community often look at Thinkific or pair Teachable with a separate tool.

**Mobile.** Teachable lists student apps for iOS and Android. Thinkific offers a branded app as a paid add-on.

**Growth path.** Thinkific Plus adds enterprise-style features such as SSO, SCORM, learning paths, and credentialing — useful if you sell to companies or run professional training. Teachable offers a custom tier for larger businesses as well.

**The rule of thumb:** if structured learning, communities, and avoiding platform transaction fees matter most, start with Thinkific. If you want built-in email and affiliate tools in a simple package and expect to use a higher plan, Teachable is a sensible choice.

## How to get the most from a 30-day free trial

A long trial is only valuable if you use it well. Here's a simple plan.

1. **Week 1 — Build one real module.** Upload videos, add text and downloads, and include a short quiz. Notice how quickly you can rearrange lessons.
2. **Week 2 — Set up selling.** Create a sales page, connect payments, and set up one pricing option plus a coupon. Buy your own course as a test.
3. **Week 3 — Test the learner experience.** Go through lessons on your phone. Check progress tracking, the certificate, and the community.
4. **Week 4 — Invite real people.** Ask a few past clients or friends to take the first module and give feedback. Their reactions tell you more than any feature list.

By the end, you'll know whether the platform fits your teaching style — before you've paid anything.

## 5 mistakes course creators make when choosing a platform

**1. Comparing subscription prices only.** Transaction fees, processing fees, and add-ons like branded apps can change the picture completely.

**2. Building everything before validating.** Pre-sell or run a pilot cohort first. Real learners tell you what to build.

**3. Ignoring the learner side.** Always test the experience as a student, especially on mobile.

**4. Forgetting about growth.** Check what higher tiers include — SSO, SCORM, learning paths, more admin seats — in case you start selling to companies.

**5. Underestimating migration.** Moving platforms later means rebuilding courses, re-enrolling students, and redoing automations. Choose with the next few years in mind.
