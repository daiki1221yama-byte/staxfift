## The real pain: your business is held together with too many tools

A typical "first course" setup often looks like this:

- A website builder for your homepage and sales page
- A separate course host for lessons
- An email tool for your list
- A payment processor and maybe a checkout tool
- A community app or private group for students
- A scheduling tool for coaching calls
- Several automation tools to make them all talk to each other

Each one is fine on its own. Together, they create problems: logins everywhere, monthly bills adding up, data that doesn't sync, and a broken link somewhere that you only discover when a student emails you, confused.

For a solo creator or a small team, every hour spent fixing tech is an hour not spent creating content or helping students. That's why the "all-in-one vs. best-of-breed" question is one of the biggest decisions you'll make.

## How to judge an online course platform: 4 criteria that matter

### 1. What's included (all-in-one vs. focused)

Some platforms aim to run your entire business — website, email, courses, community, coaching, payments, and funnels. Others do one thing extremely well and expect you to connect other tools. Neither is wrong. The question is how many tools you want to manage.

### 2. Student experience

How do lessons look on a phone? Is there a mobile app? Can students easily find their place, download resources, ask questions, and talk to each other? A good student experience means better completion rates, better testimonials, and more repeat buyers.

### 3. Selling and marketing tools

Look at checkout pages, upsells, coupons, payment plans, subscriptions, affiliate programs, email marketing, and sales funnels. If these are built in, you can launch faster.

### 4. Pricing and fees

Course platforms usually charge a monthly subscription, and some also take a transaction fee on lower plans. Many limit the number of products, contacts, or admin users per tier. Compare the total cost at your expected revenue — not just the monthly fee.

## 5 mistakes first-time course creators make

**1. Building the whole course before selling it.** Consider pre-selling or running a live pilot first. Real students tell you what they actually need — and you get paid while you build.

**2. Making it too long.** Students rarely want 40 hours of video. A shorter course that gets someone to one clear result usually sells better and gets better reviews.

**3. Ignoring the student experience on mobile.** Many students watch on their phones. Preview every lesson on a phone before launch.

**4. Choosing a platform by monthly price alone.** Factor in transaction fees, the extra tools you'll need to connect, and your own time spent maintaining integrations.

**5. Forgetting about email.** Your email list is still one of the most dependable ways to launch and sell. Whatever platform you choose, make sure you have a clear plan for collecting and nurturing leads.

## A simple 30-day launch plan

1. **Week 1 — Validate:** Survey your audience, choose one specific result your course delivers, and outline three to five modules.
2. **Week 2 — Build the essentials:** Set up your platform, create a simple sales page, and record the first module.
3. **Week 3 — Pre-sell:** Email your list and share on social media. Offer an early-bird price for founding students.
4. **Week 4 — Deliver and improve:** Release lessons, gather feedback, and collect testimonials for your next launch.

With an all-in-one platform like Kajabi, most of these steps happen in the same place, which makes a fast, focused launch much easier.

After the launch, take a week to look back before building anything new. Which emails got the most replies? Where did students get stuck? Which lessons did they finish, and which did they skip? Those answers tell you exactly what to improve, what to cut, and what your next product should be. Many creators find that their second launch, built on real feedback and real testimonials, performs far better than the first — not because the course is longer, but because it fits their students more closely. Keep a simple document of every question students ask; it quickly becomes your list of future lessons, emails, and social posts.

## How to price your first course (without overthinking it)

Pricing is where many creators freeze. A few practical principles make it much easier.

**Price the result, not the hours of video.** Students don't pay for content length; they pay for the change your course creates. A short course that helps someone land their first client is worth more than a long one that only explains theory.

**Start with a founding-student price.** For your first launch, offer a lower early price to a small group in exchange for feedback and testimonials. You get paid while you improve the course, and your next launch has social proof.

**Offer a simple ladder.** Many successful creators use three levels: a self-paced course, the course plus group coaching or community, and a premium option with one-on-one time. Even if most people choose the middle option, the ladder helps buyers understand the value.

**Consider payment plans.** Splitting a higher price into several monthly payments can make your offer accessible to more people. Most course platforms, including Kajabi, support this.

**Raise prices as you add proof.** Each round of testimonials, case studies, and improvements gives you a reason to charge more. It's much easier to start reasonable and increase than to start high and discount.

Whatever platform you choose, make sure it supports the pricing structures you have in mind — one-time payments, payment plans, subscriptions, and bundles — before you build everything.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a large audience before using Kajabi?**
Not necessarily, but it helps. Because Kajabi is premium-priced, it tends to make the most financial sense once you have some audience or an existing client base to sell to. If you're starting from zero, a simpler, lower-cost platform can be a gentler way to validate your idea first.

**Can I move my course to a different platform later?**
Usually yes, though it takes some work. Video files, written lessons, and student lists can generally be exported and re-uploaded, but things like student progress, comments, and automations often have to be rebuilt. That's one reason to choose carefully at the start.

**Is an all-in-one platform always better?**
No. All-in-one platforms save time and reduce integration headaches, but specialized tools sometimes do individual jobs better. The right answer depends on how much you enjoy setting up and maintaining tools — and how much your time is worth.

**What should I test during the free trial?**
Build one real product, its sales page, and a short email sequence. Then go through the purchase as if you were a student — ideally on your phone. If that full journey feels smooth, the platform is probably a good fit.

**Can I sell coaching and memberships, not just courses?**
On Kajabi, yes — coaching programs, communities, and subscriptions are built in. Several other platforms also support these, but the depth of each feature varies, so check the specific offer types you plan to sell.
