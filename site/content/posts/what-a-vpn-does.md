A VPN (virtual private network) is one of the most heavily advertised products online — and one of the most misunderstood. Here's an honest look at what it does, so you can decide whether it's worth paying for.

## How a VPN works, in one paragraph

Normally, your internet traffic goes from your device to your internet provider and then to the website. With a VPN, your device first creates an **encrypted tunnel** to the VPN company's server. Your provider (or the café Wi-Fi) sees only that you're connected to a VPN; the website sees the VPN server's IP address instead of yours.

## What a VPN does well

- **Protects you on public Wi-Fi.** It stops others on the same network from snooping on your traffic.
- **Hides your browsing from your internet provider.** Your ISP can't see which sites you visit.
- **Changes your apparent location.** Useful for reaching your home services while travelling — subject to each service's terms.
- **Masks your IP address** from the websites you visit.

## What a VPN does *not* do

- **It doesn't make you anonymous.** If you log in to an account, that site knows it's you.
- **It doesn't stop tracking cookies or browser fingerprinting.** Use a privacy-focused browser and settings for that.
- **It doesn't protect you from phishing or malware.** You still need to be careful what you click.
- **It moves trust, it doesn't remove it.** The VPN company can see your traffic metadata instead of your ISP. That's why the provider's policies matter.

## Do you need one?

| Situation | VPN useful? |
| --- | --- |
| You often use café, hotel or airport Wi-Fi | Yes |
| You want to keep browsing private from your ISP | Yes |
| You travel and want to access home services | Often |
| You only browse at home on a trusted network | Optional |
| You want to be fully anonymous online | Not on its own |

## How to choose a trustworthy VPN

1. **An independently audited no-logs policy.** Look for a published third-party audit, not just a promise.
2. **Modern protocols** such as WireGuard or OpenVPN.
3. **A kill switch** that blocks traffic if the VPN drops.
4. **Clear ownership and jurisdiction.** You should be able to find out who runs the company.
5. **A real refund window** so you can test speeds on your own connection.

> **Skip "free" VPNs with unclear business models.** Running servers costs money; if you're not paying, find out how the company is.

## The bottom line

A VPN is a genuinely useful tool for public Wi-Fi and everyday privacy from your ISP — but it's one layer of protection, not a force field. Pair it with a password manager and two-factor authentication for real security.
