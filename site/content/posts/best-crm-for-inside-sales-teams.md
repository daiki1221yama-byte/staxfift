If your reps spend the day jumping between a dialer, an inbox, a spreadsheet, and a CRM that nobody updates, you're paying for that friction in missed follow-ups. Picking the **best CRM for inside sales teams** isn't about the longest feature list. It's about which tool lets a rep make the next call, send the next email, and log both without thinking about it.

This guide puts Close, a CRM built around calling, email, and SMS, side by side with seven well-known alternatives: Pipedrive, HubSpot Sales Hub, Zoho CRM, Freshsales, Salesforce Starter, Copper, and Nutshell. It's a research-based evaluation drawn from each vendor's official pricing and feature pages, help documentation, and recurring themes in public user reviews. I won't quote specific prices, because plans and amounts change and vary by billing cycle and region. Instead, I'll focus on how each tool charges, where it shines, and who it suits.

## The real pain: high-volume selling with a small team

If you lead a team of two to twenty people who sell over the phone and by email, your problems probably look like this:

- **Too many tabs.** Reps dial in one tool, email from another, and copy notes into the CRM at the end of the day — if they remember.
- **Follow-ups fall through the cracks.** A lead asks to be called back next Tuesday, and nobody does. Multiply that by a few hundred leads and you're leaking revenue every week.
- **You can't coach what you can't see.** Without call recordings and activity history in one place, it's hard to know why one rep closes twice as much as another.
- **Admin eats selling time.** Every minute spent logging calls or building reports is a minute not spent talking to buyers.
- **Cost is hard to predict.** Per-seat pricing, calling minutes, phone numbers, and add-ons can turn a "cheap" CRM into an expensive one.

A good inside sales CRM removes as much of that friction as possible. It puts communication and records in the same place, and it nudges reps toward the next action instead of waiting for them to remember.

## How to choose the best CRM for inside sales teams: 5 criteria that last

These criteria don't depend on any single feature launch, so they stay useful even as products change.

### 1. Built-in communication

Can reps call, email, and text from inside the CRM, with everything logged automatically? Native communication matters more for inside sales than almost anything else, because it decides whether your data is complete or full of gaps.

### 2. Speed of outreach

Look at how quickly a rep can work through a list. Power dialers (which dial the next number automatically) and predictive dialers (which dial several numbers and connect the rep when someone picks up) can change daily call volume dramatically. Bulk email and scheduled sends matter too.

### 3. Follow-up automation

Sequences and workflows turn "I'll follow up later" into a system. Check which plan unlocks automation, since many CRMs reserve it for mid-level tiers.

### 4. Visibility and coaching

Call recordings, activity reports, and AI summaries help managers spot what's working. For small teams, simple and accurate beats deep and complicated.

### 5. Total cost at your team size

Calculate the full monthly cost for your actual headcount: seats, calling usage, phone numbers, SMS registration, and add-ons. A tool that looks affordable for one user can look very different for ten.

### Close, in plain English

Close is a CRM designed for teams whose day revolves around conversations. Instead of treating calling and email as integrations, it builds them into the core product. A rep can open a lead, call them, send a follow-up email or text, and see the full history of every touch on one screen.

Here's what stands out from Close's official pricing page:

- **Built-in calling on every plan.** Reps can make and receive calls worldwide from inside Close. Calling is billed by usage, and phone lines are a small monthly charge.
- **Faster dialing on higher tiers.** The power dialer is available on the Growth and Scale plans, and the predictive dialer is on Scale.
- **SMS included.** Sending and receiving texts is available on all plans. Note that texting US numbers requires A2P 10DLC registration, which is a carrier rule, not a Close-specific quirk.
- **Email built in.** Templates, snippets, and two-way email sync are on all plans. Open tracking starts on Essentials, while bulk email and scheduled sending come with Growth.
- **Automated workflows.** Multi-step follow-up workflows that combine email, SMS, and tasks are part of the Growth and Scale plans.
- **Call recording.** Recordings can be replayed directly on the lead page, with longer retention on higher tiers.
- **AI assistance.** Close's AI assistant, Chloe, is included on every plan. Higher tiers add AI lead summaries, suggested actions, and AI email rewriting. AI features use monthly credits that refresh each month.
- **Room to grow.** The Scale plan adds role-based access, lead visibility rules, custom objects, and live call coaching. All plans include API access.

There's no permanent free plan. There is a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, and the pricing page says it includes some calling and enrichment credits so you can test real conversations.

**The minor negative point:** the features that make Close special for high-volume teams — the power dialer, automated workflows, and bulk email — start on the Growth plan rather than the entry tiers. If you choose Solo or Essentials to save money, you get a solid calling CRM but miss much of the automation. And because calling is usage-based, your monthly bill moves with call volume. Budget for that before you roll it out to a full team.

### Pipedrive

Pipedrive is famous for its visual pipeline: deals sit in columns, and reps drag them forward as they progress. It's one of the easiest CRMs to learn, which matters when adoption is your biggest risk. Pricing is per seat across several tiers, with some capabilities sold as add-ons. There's no free plan, but Pipedrive's pricing page lists a 14-day free trial with no credit card required.

Pipedrive suits teams that think in deals and stages more than in call blocks. If your reps live on the phone, check how calling works on your chosen plan before committing.

### HubSpot Sales Hub

HubSpot offers a free CRM for up to two users with no credit card required, which makes it a popular first step. Paid Sales Hub tiers are priced per seat and include calling minutes and HubSpot-provided phone numbers. Basic workflows are part of Starter, and sales sequences appear on higher tiers.

HubSpot's big advantage is its ecosystem: marketing, service, and content tools all connect to the same records. The trade-off is that the most useful sales automation sits on higher tiers, and costs can climb as you add seats and hubs.

### Zoho CRM

Zoho CRM is highly customizable and part of a large family of business apps. It has a free edition for up to three users, then several paid editions priced per user. Zoho's pricing page lists workflow automation, multichannel "Cadences" for follow-up sequences, and built-in calling on some editions. A free trial is available.

Zoho fits teams that want flexibility and don't mind spending time on setup. Some users find the interface busier than more focused tools.

### Freshsales

Freshsales, from Freshworks, includes chat, email, and phone from its entry tier and adds sales sequences on the Pro tier. It's priced per user across Growth, Pro, and Enterprise tiers. The official FAQ describes a 21-day free trial with access to the full product — one of the longer trials in this list.

Freshsales is a good middle ground for teams that want built-in communication without the deeper calling focus of Close.

### Salesforce Starter

Salesforce's Starter and Pro Suites are designed to bring small teams onto the Salesforce platform without the complexity of its enterprise plans. Starter lists built-in sales flows, lead routing, and email marketing features, and Salesforce also lists a free suite. Free trials are available, though the length isn't stated on the pricing page.

Salesforce makes sense if you expect to grow into a much larger sales organization and want to stay on one platform. For a small calling team, it can feel like more system than you need.

### Copper

Copper is built for teams that live in Google Workspace. It works alongside Gmail and Google Calendar so contacts and emails flow into the CRM with little manual entry. Pricing is per seat across Basic, Professional, and Business tiers, with workflow automation and bulk email on Professional and email series on Business. Copper offers a free trial with no credit card required.

Calling is handled through integrations such as JustCall and RingCentral rather than a native dialer, so it's a better fit for relationship-driven sales than for high-volume calling.

### Nutshell

Nutshell is a friendly, straightforward CRM with optional marketing tools. It's priced per user across several tiers, with add-ons for things like two-way SMS and SMS marketing. AI features include call and meeting summaries, and sales automation is available on higher tiers. Nutshell offers a 14-day free trial, and its FAQ says it never asks for a credit card upfront.

Nutshell suits small teams that want sales and simple marketing under one roof and prefer a gentle learning curve.

## Close vs Pipedrive: the comparison most teams make

For small sales teams, the shortlist often comes down to Close and Pipedrive, so it's worth a closer look.

**Philosophy.** Pipedrive is pipeline-first: it organizes work around deals and stages. Close is communication-first: it organizes work around leads and the conversations you're having with them.

**Calling.** Close builds calling, SMS, and email into every plan, with power and predictive dialers on higher tiers. With Pipedrive, check how calling works on your chosen plan and whether add-ons are involved.

**Ease of use.** Both are known for being approachable. Pipedrive's drag-and-drop pipeline is especially intuitive for new reps. Close's lead view takes a little longer to learn but keeps everything about a lead on one screen.

**Cost structure.** Both charge per seat. Close adds usage-based calling, which keeps entry costs low but makes heavy-calling months more expensive. Pipedrive's add-ons can raise the total as you expand.

**The rule of thumb:** if your team's success depends on volume — dials per day, emails per day, fast follow-up — start with Close. If your sales cycle is longer and the main challenge is keeping track of deals, Pipedrive is a sensible choice.

### 1. "My reps are on the phone most of the day."

**Pick: Close.**

If you run an SDR or inside sales team that lives on calls, emails, and texts, Close was built for you. Native calling, a power dialer, SMS, and automated workflows mean fewer tools and cleaner data. Start the trial on a plan that includes the features you'll actually rely on, so you're testing the real experience.

If budget is tight or you're still figuring out your sales process, a free tier lets you get organized without commitment. HubSpot is great if you also plan to do marketing; Zoho is great if you want deep customization.

### 3. "Our deals are relationship-driven and we live in Gmail."

**Pick: Copper or Pipedrive.**

If your team sells through longer conversations and meetings rather than high-volume calling, a pipeline-focused CRM that fits your inbox will feel more natural.

## How to get the most out of a 14-day free trial

Two weeks goes fast. Here's a simple plan to make it count.

1. **Days 1–2: Import real leads.** Bring in a slice of your actual pipeline, not demo data. Set up your custom fields and lead statuses.
2. **Days 3–5: Make real calls.** Have one or two reps run their normal call blocks inside the CRM. Notice how logging, notes, and follow-up tasks feel.
3. **Days 6–8: Build one follow-up sequence.** Create a simple email or multi-step workflow for leads who don't answer. See how much manual work it removes.
4. **Days 9–11: Review recordings and reports.** As a manager, listen to a few calls and check activity reports. Ask yourself whether you'd coach differently with this view.
5. **Days 12–14: Calculate real cost.** Estimate seats, calling usage, and any add-ons for your full team, then compare with your current setup.

By the end, you'll know whether the CRM makes your team faster or just adds another tab.

## 5 mistakes small sales teams make when choosing a CRM

**1. Choosing for the manager instead of the reps.** If reps find the CRM slow, they'll stop using it and your reports will be wrong. Let the people who sell all day test it.

**2. Ignoring calling costs.** Seats are easy to compare. Calling minutes, phone numbers, and SMS registration are easy to forget.

**3. Buying the entry tier and missing the key feature.** Many CRMs put automation or dialers on mid-level plans. Check exactly which plan includes what you need.

**4. Over-customizing on day one.** Start with a simple pipeline and a few fields. You can add complexity once the team is using it every day.

**5. Skipping the migration plan.** Moving contacts, history, and email threads takes time. Ask about import tools before you commit.
