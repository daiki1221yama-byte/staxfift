## The real pain: narration is the slowest part of the course

If you're an instructional designer, an L&D specialist, or a video marketer on a team of two to twenty people, narration probably causes some of these headaches:

- **Every update means a re-record.** A new product name, a changed process, or a legal tweak forces you back into the recording booth, or back into a freelancer's queue.
- **Voices don't match across modules.** When different people narrate different lessons, the course feels stitched together.
- **Tricky words get mispronounced.** Product names, acronyms, drug names, and industry terms trip up both people and machines.
- **Localization is expensive.** Training for teams in other countries often means hiring a new voice for every language.
- **Your tools don't talk to each other.** Scripts live in slides, video lives in an editor, and audio files get passed around by email.
- **Licensing is unclear.** It isn't always obvious whether you're allowed to use generated audio in paid courses, ads, or client work.

A good AI voice tool fixes most of these at once. The trick is choosing one that fits the way your team actually builds content.

## How to choose the best AI voice generator for eLearning: 5 criteria that last

AI voice quality keeps improving across the board, so "it sounds human" isn't enough to decide. These five criteria stay useful no matter which models a vendor ships next.

### 1. Control over delivery

Training narration needs to be clear and steady, not dramatic. Look for control over speed, pitch, pauses, and emphasis, so you can slow down on a key definition or add a beat before a quiz question. Also check whether you can edit a single sentence without regenerating the whole script.

### 2. Pronunciation management

This one matters more for training than for almost any other use. You want a way to teach the tool how to say your product names and jargon once, then reuse that rule across every project. A shared pronunciation library saves hours of fixing the same word over and over.

### 3. Languages and dubbing

If your learners are spread across countries, check how many languages are supported, whether the same voice style is available across them, and whether the tool can translate or dub an existing English video rather than making you start over.

### 4. Fit with your workflow

Where do you build content? If it's PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, or an authoring tool, an integration or plugin can save a lot of copy-and-paste. If you need finished videos with a presenter on screen, a voice-only tool may not be enough.

### 5. Licensing, team access, and security

Check which plan includes commercial usage rights and downloads, how many people can work in a shared workspace, and whether the vendor documents security standards like SOC 2 and GDPR. Corporate training teams often get asked about these by IT or procurement before they can buy.

ElevenLabs suits teams that want very expressive voices, voice cloning, or a broad audio toolkit, and that are comfortable working in a dedicated audio studio rather than inside slides or an authoring tool. ### WellSaid

WellSaid focuses on professional voiceover for business content, and it shows in its pricing model. Paid plans give you unlimited generation and charge by download minutes, so you can experiment freely and only "spend" when you export a final file. Unused download minutes don't roll over.

The self-serve Starter and Pro plans include all English voices. Other languages and translation from English are part of Enterprise. Pronunciation tools are a strength: WellSaid lets you customize word pronunciation, offers AI phonetic suggestions, and integrates with the Oxford Dictionary for US and UK pronunciation. Its Business plan adds a team workspace and collaborator seats, and integrations include Adobe Express and Adobe Premiere Pro.

WellSaid's free plan needs no credit card and includes 3 download minutes per month without commercial rights. All paid plans include full commercial usage rights. It states SOC 2 Type 2 certification and GDPR compliance, and says customer data isn't used to train its models. It suits English-first training teams that care about consistent, polished narration.

### Speechify Studio

Speechify is widely known for its text-to-speech reading app, and Speechify Studio is its content creation side, with a voiceover studio, dubbing studio, voice changer, and AI avatars. Pricing uses Studio credits: one credit per second of voiceover, with dubbing and avatars costing more per second. Re-exporting unchanged speech is free, but changing pitch, speed, or emotion uses credits.

The free plan includes 600 credits and access to over 1,000 voices, but no commercial usage rights, and downloading MP3 files requires an upgrade. Paid plans add commercial rights. Speechify's FAQ says you can add pauses and adjust pitch, speed, volume, pronunciation, and emotion. It also offers a Google Slides voice-over plugin, which is handy for teams that build training decks in Slides.

Speechify Studio suits teams that want a big voice library and simple slide narration at a low entry point. The pricing page shows yearly plans, so check billing options before you commit.

### Synthesia

Synthesia suits teams that want finished avatar videos for onboarding or compliance training and don't want to manage separate audio and video tools. If you only need narration over your own slides or screen recordings, a voice-focused tool may be simpler and better value.

## How to get the most out of a free trial

Free tiers and trials come with limited minutes or credits, so plan your test before you sign up. This approach works for most AI voice tools.

1. **Pick one real script.** Choose a short module, three to five minutes long, that includes your product names, acronyms, and at least one tricky term.
2. **Test three voices, not thirty.** Shortlist voices that match your brand and audience. Narrate the same paragraph with each and ask a colleague to choose without knowing which is which.
3. **Fix pronunciation once.** Add your hardest terms to the pronunciation tool and check that the fix carries over to a second project.
4. **Edit like you will in real life.** Change one sentence in the middle of the script and regenerate it. See how easy it is to keep the timing and tone consistent.
5. **Try your real workflow.** If the tool integrates with your slides or authoring tool, use it. If you need another language, dub one short section and have a native speaker review it.
6. **Check the license and export.** Before you rely on any audio, confirm which plan includes downloads and commercial rights.

By the end, you'll know whether the tool will speed up your course updates or just add another step.

## 5 mistakes teams make with AI voiceover

**1. Choosing on a single demo clip.** Vendor demos are hand-picked. Always test with your own script and your own jargon.

**2. Forgetting pronunciation until the end.** Fixing one mispronounced product name in fifty lessons is painful. Set up pronunciation rules before you produce at scale.

**3. Ignoring licensing.** Free plans often exclude commercial use. Make sure the plan you pay for covers courses, client work, or ads, depending on how you'll use the audio.

**4. Over-directing the voice.** Training narration works best when it's calm and clear. Too much emphasis and emotion can feel like an ad and distract learners.

**5. Skipping native-speaker review.** AI dubbing is fast, but a quick review by a native speaker catches awkward phrasing before your learners do.
