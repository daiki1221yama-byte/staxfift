A webinar can be your best lead source or a polite waste of an afternoon. The difference often comes down to things that happen around the live session: how easy it is to register, whether people actually show up, how engaged they are, and whether their data lands in your CRM before your sales team moves on. Choosing the **best webinar platform for B2B marketing** means choosing the tool that makes all of that smooth, for you and for your attendees.

This guide compares Livestorm, a browser-based webinar and video engagement platform, with five popular alternatives: Zoom Webinars, GoTo Webinar, Demio, WebinarJam, and Riverside. It's a research-based evaluation built from each vendor's official pricing and product pages. I haven't run a full webinar program on every one of these tools, so I'll stick to what each vendor documents and explain what it means for a small B2B team. I also won't quote specific prices, because amounts change and depend on plan, audience size, billing cycle, and region. Instead, I'll focus on how each tool charges, what it includes, and who it suits.

## The real pain: webinars that take a lot and give back too little

If you're a marketer or customer success manager at a B2B company with somewhere between five and fifty people, your webinar program probably runs into some of these problems:

- **Attendees struggle to join.** A download prompt or a confusing link five minutes before start time is enough to lose a busy prospect.
- **No-shows are common.** Plenty of people register and never attend. Without good reminders and replays, those leads go cold.
- **Engagement is flat.** A one-way slideshow with a silent chat box doesn't move anyone closer to buying.
- **Data gets stuck.** Registrations and attendance live in the webinar tool while sales works in the CRM. Someone ends up exporting and importing spreadsheets.
- **Repeating the same demo is draining.** Running the same product demo live every week takes hours that could go into new content.
- **Pricing is hard to compare.** Some tools charge by host, some by attendee capacity, some by attendees who actually show up. Comparing them fairly takes effort.

A good webinar platform solves as many of these as it can, so a small team can run a steady program without burning out.

## How to choose the best webinar platform for B2B marketing: 5 criteria that last

These criteria don't depend on any one product update, so they stay useful as tools change.

### 1. Attendee experience

Can people join directly from a browser without installing anything? Are registration pages clean and easy to customize? Every bit of friction costs you attendees.

### 2. Live, on-demand, and automated options

B2B teams often need all three: live sessions for launches and Q&A, on-demand replays for people who missed it, and automated or pre-recorded sessions for evergreen demos.

### 3. Engagement tools

Polls, Q&A, chat, reactions, and the ability to share links or files keep people involved. These features also give you signals about who is interested.

### 4. CRM and marketing integrations

Check whether the tool connects with the CRM and marketing automation platform you already use, such as HubSpot, Salesforce, or Marketo, and which plan includes that connection.

### 5. Pricing model at your size

Decide whether pricing by host, by attendee capacity, or by actual attendees works best for your program. A team running many small sessions and a team running a few large ones will get very different value from the same model.

### Livestorm, in plain English

Livestorm is a video engagement platform for webinars, virtual events, and product demos. It runs in the browser, so attendees can join without downloading software, and it's built to handle the whole webinar journey, from registration page to follow-up email.

Here's what stands out from Livestorm's official pricing page, homepage, and support documentation:

- **Three plans: Free, Pro, and Enterprise.** Pro and Enterprise use attendee-based pricing. One attendee credit is used for each unique participant per session, including people who join live or watch a replay or on-demand session. Registrations, no-shows, and team members don't use credits.
- **Browser-based attendance.** The pricing page says participants connect directly from a browser without downloading software.
- **Live, on-demand, and automated events.** You can create on-demand events people can watch anytime, automate the start and end of sessions, or automatically play a video. Live sessions can be converted to on-demand.
- **Registration and emails.** Customizable registration pages with custom fields, an embeddable widget, and unlimited customized reminder and follow-up emails.
- **Engagement.** Chat, polls, a Q&A tab with question upvotes, emoji reactions, raise hand, and sharing of files, media, and links.
- **Analytics.** Attendance rate, a webinar conversion rate that shows how many attendees took your desired action, chat and question exports, and UTM tracking.
- **Integrations.** HubSpot and Zapier are part of the standard integrations. Salesforce, Marketo, Pardot, Microsoft Dynamics, and Brevo are labeled as Enterprise-only.
- **Recordings and branding.** Recording controls, instant replays, unlimited replay storage, and custom branding for registration pages, emails, and the event room.

The Free plan doesn't require a credit card. According to Livestorm's support documentation, it includes a small yearly allowance of attendee credits, events of up to 30 minutes, and a limited number of events, with premium features turned off. It's enough to test the experience, not to run a full program.

**The minor negative point:** Livestorm's pricing works well for some teams and less well for others. Pro and Enterprise are billed annually, the support documentation says monthly plans aren't available, and unused attendee credits don't roll over to the next billing period. If your webinar schedule is irregular, estimate your yearly attendance carefully. Also note that Salesforce and Marketo integrations are Enterprise-only, so teams on those systems should factor that in.

### Zoom Webinars

Zoom is the name most attendees already know, which can make joining feel familiar. Its webinar and events pricing is based on attendee capacity, with Webinars, Webinars Plus, and Events plans, and pay-per-attendee options on the higher tiers. Zoom says attendees don't need a Zoom account and can join with one-click links. Engagement tools include chat, Q&A, emoji reactions, polls and quizzes, and post-webinar surveys. Webinars Plus lets you pre-record content and run it like it's live.

Integrations with HubSpot, Salesforce, Marketo, Pardot, and Eloqua are listed on all tiers, which is generous for teams on larger marketing stacks. Zoom's events pricing page doesn't mention a free trial. It suits teams that want a familiar brand and broad integrations, especially for large audiences.

### GoTo Webinar

GoTo Webinar is a long-established webinar tool. Its plans (Reach, Elevate, and Complete) differ mainly by participant cap and organizer seats. Reach includes reporting and analytics, automated email reminders and follow-ups, and polls and live Q&A. Elevate adds branded webinars and custom URLs, cloud recordings, AI summaries and transcripts, breakout rooms, and the ability to run recordings as live events. Complete adds paid events, certified training, and registration source tracking.

GoTo's trial page describes a free trial with no credit card or commitment required. It suits teams that want a structured, traditional webinar tool with clear tiers.

### Demio

Demio is built for marketing webinars. Pricing is per host, with attendee room sizes as the main variable. Chat, polls, featured actions, handouts, and engagement analytics are on all plans. Pre-recorded, on-demand, and automated webinars, registration pages with custom fields, and custom branding come with Growth and Premium. Premium integrations with HubSpot, Salesforce, Marketo, and Pardot are on the Premium plan.

Demio offers a 14-day free trial that includes Growth features, limited to 20 attendees and one-hour sessions. It suits marketing teams that want a focused webinar tool with strong calls to action.

### WebinarJam

WebinarJam is popular with course creators and marketers who sell directly from webinars. Pricing is in flat tiers capped by attendees per webinar, with more hosts and longer sessions on higher tiers. Its homepage says attendees join instantly from their browser or device. Features include live chat, polls and surveys, live offer displays, a whiteboard, and automatic replays. Automated webinars are supported, and its sister product EverWebinar is included on Basic and higher plans.

The pricing page invites you to try it free for 14 days and mentions a 30-day money-back guarantee. Its integrations lean toward email marketing tools like AWeber, ActiveCampaign, and Keap. It suits teams that sell during webinars more than teams focused on CRM-driven B2B pipelines.

### Riverside

Riverside is best known for high-quality recording, and its Webinar plan brings that to live events. The Webinar plan includes registration forms, branded email reminders and follow-ups, Q&A, live call-ins, polls and reactions, webinar analytics, and a HubSpot integration. You can also pre-record a session and stream it as live. The Business plan raises the registrant cap and adds Salesforce and Marketo integrations.

Riverside has a free plan for recording, and paid plans offer a 14-day free trial. It suits teams that care about production quality and want to reuse webinar recordings as podcasts or social clips.

## Livestorm vs Zoom Webinars: the comparison most teams make

For many B2B teams, the shortlist comes down to Livestorm and Zoom Webinars. Here's how they differ.

**Attendee experience.** Livestorm runs in the browser and states that no download is needed. Zoom says attendees don't need a Zoom account and can join with one-click links. Both aim to make joining easy; Livestorm's whole product is designed around the browser.

**Pricing model.** Livestorm charges by attendee credits, so you pay for unique people who actually join or watch, not for registrations. Zoom prices by attendee capacity, with pay-per-attendee options on higher tiers. If your no-show rate is high, Livestorm's model can be easier on the budget. If you run very large events, Zoom's capacity tiers may be more predictable.

**Integrations.** Zoom lists HubSpot, Salesforce, Marketo, Pardot, and Eloqua on all tiers. Livestorm includes HubSpot and Zapier on Pro but reserves Salesforce and Marketo for Enterprise.

**On-demand and automation.** Livestorm includes on-demand events, automation, and live-to-on-demand conversion. Zoom offers pre-recorded sessions run like live on Webinars Plus.

**The rule of thumb:** if you're a HubSpot team running regular demos and want a polished browser experience with replays and on-demand content, start with Livestorm. If you rely on Salesforce or Marketo and need large audiences on a smaller budget, compare Zoom carefully.

## How to get the most out of a free plan or trial

Free plans and trials come with limits, so plan before you start. This approach works for most webinar tools.

1. **Day 1: Build a real registration page.** Use your branding, add the custom fields your sales team needs, and connect your CRM if your plan allows it.
2. **Days 2–3: Set up reminders and follow-ups.** Write a reminder sequence and a follow-up email that includes the replay. These emails drive much of your attendance.
3. **Days 4–5: Run a short internal rehearsal.** Invite colleagues, join from different browsers and devices, and test polls, Q&A, and screen sharing.
4. **Days 6–8: Host a small live session.** Keep it short and focused on one topic. Watch the attendance rate and engagement data afterward.
5. **Days 9–10: Turn it into on-demand content.** Convert the recording into an on-demand or automated session and share it with people who didn't attend.
6. **Final days: Calculate real cost.** Estimate how many sessions you'll run and how many people will attend over a year, then compare pricing models across tools.

By the end, you'll know whether the platform helps you generate pipeline or just hosts video calls.

## 5 mistakes B2B teams make with webinar software

**1. Ignoring the joining experience.** A download prompt or a confusing link loses people at the last minute. Test joining from a fresh browser before every major event.

**2. Treating replays as an afterthought.** Many registrants never attend live. A good replay and on-demand strategy can reach more people than the live session.

**3. Not connecting the CRM.** If webinar data doesn't reach sales quickly, hot leads cool down. Set up integrations before your first public event.

**4. Running the same demo live forever.** Once a demo is polished, consider automating it or offering it on demand, and save live sessions for Q&A and launches.

**5. Choosing on headline price alone.** Compare pricing models against your real program: number of sessions, typical attendance, and no-show rate.
