## The real pain: marketing without a tech team

If you're a coach, consultant, or small business owner running things with a handful of people (or on your own), these problems probably sound familiar:

- **Your website isn't built to convert.** A homepage tries to say everything. A landing page says one thing and asks for one action. Most small business sites don't have that.
- **You can't wait on a developer.** By the time someone builds the page, the promotion is over.
- **Tools don't talk to each other.** Your form needs to reach your email platform, your calendar, and sometimes your CRM. Every broken connection costs leads.
- **You don't know what's working.** Without testing, you're guessing which headline or offer brings in more sign-ups.
- **Pricing is confusing.** Some tools charge by visitors, some by contacts, some by pages or sites. The cheapest-looking plan isn't always the cheapest for you.

A good landing page builder fixes most of this. It gives you templates that already follow conversion basics, a simple editor, and integrations that move leads to the right place automatically.

## 5 criteria for choosing the best landing page builder for small business

These criteria don't depend on any specific feature launch, so they stay useful as tools evolve.

### 1. Ease of building

Can a non-designer pick a template, change the text and images, and publish without breaking the layout? Look for a drag-and-drop editor, mobile previews, and AI help for first drafts.

### 2. Conversion tools

Forms, popups, and clear calls to action are the basics. A/B testing lets you compare two versions of a page. More advanced tools route each visitor to the version most likely to convert for them.

### 3. How pricing scales

This is where tools differ most. Some cap monthly visitors, some cap contacts, some limit the number of pages or sites. Think about where your traffic is heading, not just where it is today.

### 4. Integrations and domains

Check that the builder connects to your email tool, calendar, and payment processor, and that you can publish on your own domain.

### 5. Room to grow

Will you need a full website, a blog, a sales funnel, or extra team seats later? Choosing a tool you can grow into avoids a painful migration.

### Leadpages, in plain English

Leadpages is a landing page and small website builder made for people who want results without technical work. You pick a template or let AI generate a draft, edit it in a drag-and-drop editor, and publish on your own domain. It's long been popular with coaches, consultants, and small businesses because it keeps the focus on capturing leads.

Here's what stands out from Leadpages' official pricing page:

- **Unlimited visitors on every plan.** The pricing page says there are no traffic caps. That's a big deal if a post goes viral or an ad campaign takes off, because your bill doesn't jump with traffic.
- **Pages, sites, and blogs.** Every paid plan includes the drag-and-drop editor, AI page generation, sites and blogs, and custom domains with free SSL. Lower plans limit the number of pages and sites; higher plans remove the page limit.
- **AI credits included.** Each plan comes with a monthly allowance of AI credits for generating and editing content.
- **Testing and personalization on higher plans.** A/B testing starts on the Grow plan (two-way tests), and the Optimize plan allows more variants. Optimize also adds an AI traffic-routing feature Leadpages calls Smart Traffic, plus auto-personalization and heatmaps.
- **Dynamic text replacement.** From the Grow plan, pages can change text to match the ad or keyword a visitor came from.
- **Lead tools.** Grow and above add lead enrichment and anonymous traffic detection.
- **Team options.** Higher plans include more seats, and extra seats can be added.
- **Developer-friendly extras.** REST API access is included on paid plans.

Leadpages offers a 7-day trial on its paid plans. According to the pricing page, a card is required, a small trial fee is charged and counted toward your first month, and billing starts when the trial ends unless you cancel.

**The minor negative point:** the trial is short compared with competitors that offer two weeks, and it needs a card. Also, A/B testing and the more advanced optimization tools aren't on the entry plans. If testing is a priority from day one, budget for a mid-level plan — or plan your trial carefully so you can judge the editor and templates in a week.

### Unbounce

Unbounce is built for marketers who run paid ads and care about every percentage point of conversion. Its plans are capped by monthly traffic, users, and domains, with unlimited conversions. The entry plan is limited to a small number of pages, while higher plans unlock unlimited pages, A/B testing, dynamic text replacement, and on the Optimize plan, AI traffic routing (Smart Traffic). There's a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, though the trial has limits such as a single user and no custom domains.

Unbounce is excellent if you run steady paid traffic and want to test aggressively. For a solo coach with modest traffic, the testing depth may be more than you need.

### Instapage

Instapage targets ad teams and agencies with features like real-time collaboration, server-side A/B testing, and ad-to-page personalization on its top tier. Plans are based on unique monthly visitors, with unlimited pages and conversions. It also includes popups, sticky bars, a form builder, and AI content tools. There's a 14-day free trial on the self-serve plans, but the pricing page says you need to enter a credit card to start it.

Instapage is a strong fit for teams with real ad budgets. For a one-person business, its strengths can feel oversized.

### Systeme.io

Systeme.io is an all-in-one platform: funnels, email marketing, a blog, and payments in one place. Its plans are based on contacts rather than visitors, email sends are unlimited, and the pricing page says there are no transaction fees on any plan. Most importantly for tight budgets, there's a free plan that never expires and doesn't require a credit card.

Systeme.io is a great starting point if you want funnels and email together. The trade-off is that its page editor is generally seen as less flexible than dedicated landing page builders.

### Carrd

Carrd is a simple, affordable builder for one-page sites. You can create a free account, and the paid Pro plans are billed yearly and priced by the number of sites. Forms, custom domains, and payment embeds (such as Stripe or PayPal) are available on Pro plans, and you can try Pro free for 7 days without a credit card.

Carrd is ideal for a quick personal page, link-in-bio site, or simple waitlist. It doesn't list A/B testing or advanced conversion tools.

### ClickFunnels

ClickFunnels is built around complete sales funnels: landing pages, checkout, upsells, email marketing, and automations. Its plans include unlimited funnels, visitors, and pages, with workspaces, team members, and contacts scaling by tier. A/B testing works on funnels and email campaigns, and there's an AI funnel builder. A 14-day free trial is listed.

ClickFunnels suits businesses that sell directly from their pages. If you only need lead-capture pages, it may be more platform than you need.

### Landingi

Landingi is a feature-rich builder with templates, a form builder, popups, an AI page generator, and server-side A/B testing on mid tiers. Plans are based on active landing pages and monthly visits, and the higher tier adds client sub-accounts and agency branding. There's a 14-day free trial for every plan; the pricing page notes that a card is sometimes requested and that trial visits are limited.

Landingi works well for small marketing teams and freelancers who want lots of features at each tier.

## Leadpages vs Unbounce: the comparison most small businesses make

These two often end up on the same shortlist, so here's a closer look.

**Pricing logic.** This is the biggest difference. Leadpages advertises unlimited visitors on every plan and limits pages and sites on lower tiers. Unbounce caps monthly traffic by plan and lists unlimited conversions. If your traffic spikes unpredictably, Leadpages' model is easier to budget.

**Testing.** Both put A/B testing on mid-level plans and AI traffic routing on higher plans. Unbounce leans more heavily into experimentation as its core identity.

**Websites.** Leadpages includes sites and blogs alongside landing pages, which is handy if you want one tool for a small business site. Unbounce focuses on landing pages for campaigns.

**Trial.** Unbounce offers a longer trial with no card. Leadpages' trial is shorter and requires a card.

**The rule of thumb:** if you're a small business that wants lead-capture pages and a simple site with predictable costs, start with Leadpages. If you're running steady paid campaigns and live in A/B tests, Unbounce is worth a serious look.

## How to get the most out of a 7-day free trial

A week is enough if you go in with a plan.

1. **Day 1: Pick one goal.** Choose a single page you actually need — an opt-in for your lead magnet, a booking page, or a webinar sign-up.
2. **Day 2: Build from a template.** Start with a template close to your goal. Swap in your headline, image, and offer. Try the AI generator for a first draft if you're stuck.
3. **Day 3: Connect your tools.** Link your email platform and calendar. Submit the form yourself to confirm leads arrive where they should.
4. **Day 4: Publish on your domain.** Set up your custom domain and check the page on your phone.
5. **Days 5–6: Send real traffic.** Share the page with your email list or social followers. Even a little traffic shows you how it performs.
6. **Day 7: Decide.** Look at sign-ups, ease of editing, and which plan you'd need. Cancel before billing starts if it's not the right fit.

## 5 mistakes small businesses make when choosing a landing page builder

**1. Choosing on price alone.** A plan that limits traffic or pages can cost more than expected once your marketing works.

**2. Forgetting about integrations.** If leads don't reach your email tool automatically, you'll lose them. Test the connection first.

**3. Overbuilding the first page.** One clear headline, one offer, and one button beat a long page full of ideas.

**4. Skipping mobile checks.** Many visitors arrive on phones. Always preview and test on mobile.

**5. Never testing.** Even simple tests — a different headline or button text — can show you what your audience responds to. Make sure your plan supports testing when you're ready.
