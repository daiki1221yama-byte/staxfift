There are dozens of budgeting apps, and most look similar in screenshots. The difference shows up after a few weeks of use. These are the features that decide whether you'll stick with one.

## First, pick your budgeting style

| Method | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero-based | Every dollar gets a job before you spend it | Hands-on planners, paying off debt |
| 50/30/20 | Needs, wants, savings split by percentage | People who want a simple rule |
| Tracking only | Record spending, review trends | Getting started, low effort |
| Envelope | Fixed amounts per category | Controlling overspending |

Choose an app that supports **your** method instead of forcing a new one on you.

## The 7 features that matter

### 1. Reliable bank syncing

Automatic import of transactions saves hours. Check that your banks and cards are supported and that connections don't break constantly.

### 2. Smart categorization you can correct

Good apps learn from your edits, so you fix "Coffee shop → Dining" once, not every week.

### 3. Goals and savings targets

Seeing progress toward an emergency fund or a trip keeps you motivated.

### 4. Bill and subscription tracking

Forgotten subscriptions are one of the easiest leaks to fix.

### 5. Shared budgets

If you manage money with a partner, shared access matters more than any other feature.

### 6. Privacy and security

Look for read-only bank connections, two-factor login and a clear privacy policy on how your data is used.

### 7. Easy export

You should be able to download your data as CSV at any time.

> **Don't overlook the free option.** A simple spreadsheet works brilliantly for many people. Pay for an app only if automation will save you real time.

## The bottom line

The best budgeting app is the one that matches your method and that you'll open every week. Start with a free trial, connect your accounts, and see whether you're still using it a month later.
