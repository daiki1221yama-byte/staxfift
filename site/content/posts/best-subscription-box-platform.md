Choosing the **best subscription box platform** decides whether those moments feel automatic or eat your whole week.

## The real pain: running recurring orders with a tiny team

If you're a founder or part of a small team of one to ten people, launching or growing a subscription box, your days probably include some of this:

- **Too many moving parts.** A store builder, a subscription app, a payment processor, an email tool, and a spreadsheet for this month's box contents — all stitched together.
- **Churn you can't see coming.** Subscribers cancel quietly, and you only notice when revenue dips.
- **Failed payments.** Expired cards and declined charges cost real money every billing cycle if nobody follows up.
- **Gift season chaos.** Gift subscriptions have different rules: a buyer, a recipient, a fixed number of boxes, and a message card.
- **Customer service overload.** "Can I skip next month?" "Can I change my address?" "Can I swap one item?" If customers can't do it themselves, you do it for them.
- **Fees that stack up.** A monthly platform fee, an app fee, transaction fees, and payment processing can add up to more than you expected.

The right platform makes the common requests self-service, catches failed payments, and gives you early warning on churn, so you can spend your time on the product inside the box.

## How to choose the best subscription box platform: 5 criteria that last

These criteria don't depend on any single feature launch, so they stay useful as products change.

### 1. All-in-one or add-on?

Some platforms are a complete store with subscriptions built in. Others are apps that add subscriptions to an existing store on Shopify or WordPress. Neither is better by default. If you already have a busy store, an add-on keeps everything in one place. If you're starting fresh, an all-in-one platform can save setup time.

### 2. Subscriber self-service

A good customer portal lets subscribers skip, pause, swap products, update payment details, and change addresses without emailing you. This one feature can save hours every week.

### 3. Churn and payment recovery tools

Look for cancellation flows (asking why people leave and offering a pause or discount), failed-payment retries, and win-back campaigns. Small improvements in retention compound month after month.

### 4. Subscription box specifics

Boxes have needs that simple "subscribe and save" doesn't cover: build-a-box, gift subscriptions, fixed shipping dates, commitment periods, and prepaid plans. Check that the features you need are on the plan you can afford.

### 5. Total cost at your volume

Most platforms combine a monthly fee with a transaction fee, and some add payment processing on top. Estimate your monthly order value, then calculate the full cost on each platform at your size and at the size you hope to reach.

### Subbly, in plain English

Subbly is a complete platform for selling subscriptions: website, hosting, checkout, subscription billing, and customer portal in one product. Its pricing page makes a point of saying there's no app to bolt on, on any plan. That's the core difference from most alternatives here, which add subscriptions to a store you already run.

- **A churn suite.** Cancellation reasons, retention offers, and commitment periods are built in, along with predictive churn, surveys, and funnels. That's a strong set of retention tools for a small team.

### Recharge

Recharge is one of the best-known subscription apps for Shopify stores. It works inside Shopify's checkout and supports Shopify discounts, tax, and shipping natively. Pricing combines a fixed monthly fee with usage-based transaction fees, and the Starter plan comes with a 60-day free trial — the longest trial in this list.

Recharge's feature list is broad: a product-page subscription widget, a no-code customer portal, cancellation prevention, failed-payment recovery, win-backs, and prepaid subscriptions on all tiers. Bundles and surprise-and-delight gifting are on the higher Scale tier. It suits established Shopify brands that want a proven, widely used option.

### Skio

Skio is a Shopify subscription app that positions itself as an alternative to Recharge, with a support team that includes former Shopify and Recharge staff. Pricing combines a monthly fee with a transaction fee on subscription orders, and there's a custom Enterprise plan.

Its Scale plan includes a lot: a multi-step cancel flow builder, payment recovery, lifecycle automation, built-in SMS, build-a-box, gifting, and a migration service. It's a strong pick for growing Shopify brands that want many retention tools in a single plan, though loyalty features cost extra.

### Loop Subscriptions

Loop is another Shopify subscription app with transparent pricing: a monthly plan fee plus a percentage of transaction volume, with no per-order flat fee. Its FAQ describes a free plan for a small number of active subscriptions and a 14-day trial on paid plans, which are billed month-to-month.

Loop includes failed-payment recovery, cancellation prevention, a self-serve portal, and subscription bundles from its entry tier. Prepaid subscriptions, gifting, and win-back campaigns are on the Pro tier. White-glove migration is included on every tier, which lowers the cost of switching.

### Shopify Subscriptions

Shopify's own subscription app is free to install. It covers the basics: automatic billing on a weekly, monthly, or yearly schedule, subscription discounts, and the ability for customers to cancel, skip, or pause. It also works with Shopify POS.

It's a sensible first step for a Shopify store testing whether subscriptions will work. For a dedicated box business, you may outgrow it once you need build-a-box, deep churn tools, or advanced gifting.

### WooCommerce Subscriptions

WooCommerce Subscriptions is an official extension for WordPress stores, sold as an annual license with a 30-day money-back guarantee. It supports subscription and one-time purchase options, free trials and sign-up fees for your customers, gifting, automatic payment retries, and upgrades or downgrades with proration. Subscribers manage their plans in their account area.

It suits founders who already run WordPress and want full control. The trade-off is that you handle hosting, updates, and plugin compatibility yourself.

### Cratejoy

Cratejoy is best known today as a consumer marketplace for subscription boxes, where shoppers browse boxes from independent sellers and buy them as gifts. Its homepage also links to a seller program and a site builder. The marketplace can help with discovery, but seller pricing and terms aren't shown on the main page, so check the seller site before deciding. Many founders use a marketplace listing alongside their own store rather than instead of it.

## Questions to ask before you migrate existing subscribers

If you already have subscribers on another platform, switching is the riskiest part of the decision. Ask these questions before you sign up, whichever platform you're moving to.

**Can my subscribers' payment details move with them?** The best migrations transfer saved payment tokens securely between processors, so customers don't have to re-enter their cards. If they do, expect some to drop off. Ask exactly which payment processors are supported for migration.

**Will billing dates stay the same?** Subscribers expect to be charged on the same day they always have been. Confirm that next-charge dates, skipped months, and paused subscriptions carry over correctly.

**What happens to gift and prepaid subscriptions?** These have special rules, such as a fixed number of remaining boxes. Make sure they don't accidentally turn into ongoing monthly charges.

**Who does the work?** Some platforms include hands-on migration help, while others give you import tools and documentation. Know which you're getting, and how long it typically takes.

**How will I tell customers?** Even a smooth migration changes the login page and emails your subscribers see. Plan a short, friendly message explaining what's changing and what stays the same.

**Can I run both systems briefly?** A short overlap lets you check the first billing cycle on the new platform before you shut the old one down.

Getting clear answers to these questions upfront turns a stressful move into a planned project.

## 5 mistakes new subscription box founders make

**1. Ignoring fees at scale.** A small percentage feels harmless at 50 subscribers. At 2,000, it's a real line item. Model both.

**2. Hiding the cancel button.** Making cancellation hard creates angry customers and chargebacks. A friendly flow with a pause option keeps more people.

**3. Forgetting failed payments.** Without automatic retries and reminders, you lose subscribers who never meant to leave.

**4. Launching without gifting.** For many boxes, gifts drive a big share of sales. Set gifting up before your first busy season.

**5. Choosing on features alone.** Think about where you'll be in a year. Moving thousands of subscribers between platforms is possible but stressful.
