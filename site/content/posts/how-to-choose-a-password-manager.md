If you reuse passwords, one leaked account can unlock the rest. A password manager fixes that by creating and remembering a unique, strong password for every site. Here's how to pick one quickly.

## Must-have features

- **End-to-end encryption.** Your vault should be encrypted on your device so the company can't read it.
- **Apps for every device you use** — phone, computer and browser extensions.
- **Autofill that works reliably** on the sites and apps you use daily.
- **Two-factor authentication** to protect the vault itself.
- **Easy import and export,** so you're never locked in.

## Nice-to-haves

- Secure sharing with family or teammates
- Breach alerts for compromised passwords
- Passkey support
- Secure notes and document storage

## Built-in vs. dedicated password managers

| | Built into browser/phone | Dedicated password manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Free | Free tier or paid |
| Works across all browsers & platforms | Sometimes limited | Yes |
| Sharing with family | Limited | Usually yes |
| Extra features | Basic | Breach alerts, secure notes, more |

The built-in option is far better than reusing passwords. A dedicated manager is worth it if you use several platforms or want to share logins safely.

## How to switch in an afternoon

1. Install the password manager on your computer and phone.
2. Import passwords saved in your browser.
3. Turn **off** your browser's built-in password saving to avoid duplicates.
4. Change your most important passwords first: email, banking, and anything with payment details.
5. Enable two-factor authentication on the vault and on your email account.

> **Your master password is the one to remember.** Make it a long passphrase — four or more random words — and never use it anywhere else.

## The bottom line

Any reputable password manager is a big step up. Choose the one whose apps feel easiest to you — the best password manager is the one you'll actually use every day.
