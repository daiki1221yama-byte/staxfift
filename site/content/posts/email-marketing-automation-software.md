Your email list is probably the most valuable thing you own online. And yet most small businesses use it the same way: one newsletter, sent to everyone, whenever someone remembers.

Good **email marketing automation software** changes that. It sends the right message to the right person at the right moment — a welcome series for new subscribers, a nudge for someone who visited your pricing page, a check-in for a customer who hasn't opened anything in a while — without you pressing "send" each time.

In this guide, I'll walk through how I'd choose an automation platform for a small online business, then compare ActiveCampaign with six well-known alternatives: Mailchimp, Brevo, MailerLite, Kit (formerly ConvertKit), Klaviyo, and HubSpot Marketing Hub. This comparison is based on public documentation, feature pages, help centers, and consistent patterns in user reviews. ## The real pain: your list is growing, but your emails aren't working harder

Here's what usually happens as a small business grows its list:

- **Everyone gets the same email.** A brand-new subscriber and a three-year customer receive identical messages, even though they need very different things.
- **Follow-ups depend on memory.** Someone downloads your guide, books a call, or abandons a cart — and whether they hear from you again depends on whether you notice.
- **Data lives in separate tools.** Your website, store, booking tool, and email platform each know a little about the customer, but nothing connects them.
- **You can't tell what's working.** Opens and clicks are visible, but it's hard to see which emails actually lead to sales.

With a small team, you can't fix this by hiring. You fix it by setting up a few smart automations once and letting them run.

## How to judge email automation software: 4 criteria that matter

### 1. Automation depth

Basic tools can send a welcome series. Better tools let you build branching journeys: *if they clicked this link, send that; if they bought, stop the sequence; if they haven't opened in 30 days, try a different message.* Look for a visual builder, conditions, goals, and the ability to trigger automations from website visits, purchases, form fills, or tags.

### 2. Segmentation and personalization

Can you easily create groups based on behavior, interests, purchase history, or custom fields? Can you change parts of an email depending on who's reading it? Good segmentation is what turns "newsletter" into "relevant message."

### 3. Ease of use

Powerful features don't help if building a simple campaign takes an hour. Check the email editor, the template library, and how understandable the automation builder feels on your first try.

### 4. Pricing as your list grows

Most platforms charge by number of contacts; some charge by emails sent; a few by seats or features. A tool that's cheap at 1,000 contacts can become expensive at 25,000. Look at your expected list size a year from now, not just today.

### Mailchimp

Mailchimp is the email tool most people have heard of. It's easy to send a nice-looking campaign, and its templates and editor are beginner-friendly. Automation has improved over time, but it's generally less flexible than ActiveCampaign for complex, branching journeys. Many users also note that costs can rise quickly as lists grow. It's a fine choice for simple newsletters and basic sequences.

### Brevo

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) combines email, SMS, and chat, and stands out for **pricing mainly by email volume rather than by contacts.** That can be very cost-effective if you have a large list but send infrequently. Its automation is solid for common workflows, though not as deep as ActiveCampaign's.

### MailerLite

MailerLite is loved for its simplicity. The editor is clean, landing pages and forms are easy, and its free plan is generous for small lists. Automations cover the essentials well. If your needs are straightforward and you want something calm and affordable, it's hard to beat — just expect fewer advanced options as you grow.

### Kit (formerly ConvertKit)

Kit is built for creators: writers, podcasters, course creators, and newsletter publishers. Its tag-based system is simple to understand, its visual automations are friendly, and it includes creator-focused features like paid newsletters and recommendations. It's less suited to complex B2B sales processes, but for audience-first businesses it's an excellent fit.

### Klaviyo

Klaviyo is the go-to for many e-commerce brands, especially on Shopify. It pulls in detailed purchase and browsing data and makes it easy to build flows like abandoned cart, post-purchase, and win-back. If you run an online store, it's often the strongest choice. For service businesses or creators, it can feel like more than you need.

### HubSpot Marketing Hub

HubSpot offers a full marketing suite — email, landing pages, forms, ads, social, and a CRM — in one connected system. It's polished and easy to navigate, and its free tools are a good start. The tradeoff is cost: the more advanced automation features live in the higher tiers, which are priced for larger teams.

## 5 automations every small business should set up first

Whichever platform you choose, these five automations tend to deliver the most value for the least effort.

**1. Welcome series (3–5 emails).** Introduce yourself, deliver what you promised, share your best content, and gently present your offer. This is often your highest-performing sequence.

**2. Lead magnet follow-up.** When someone downloads a guide or checklist, follow up with tips on using it, then invite them to the next step.

**3. Abandoned action reminder.** Abandoned cart, unfinished booking, or a started-but-not-submitted form — a friendly reminder recovers more than most people expect.

**4. Post-purchase onboarding.** Help new customers get value quickly. Happy customers buy again and refer others.

**5. Re-engagement.** Contacts who haven't opened in a long time get a "still interested?" message. Those who don't respond can be removed, keeping your list healthy and your costs down.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Building a huge automation first.** Start with one simple journey and improve it. Complex maps you never finish don't help anyone.
- **Ignoring deliverability.** Authenticate your sending domain, avoid buying lists, and remove unengaged contacts. Good automation doesn't matter if emails land in spam.
- **Too many tags.** Agree on a small, clear tagging system early. Messy tags make segmentation confusing later.
- **Not measuring revenue.** Opens and clicks are nice, but connect your platform to your store or CRM so you can see which emails actually lead to sales.

## Writing automated emails people actually read

Automation only works if the emails themselves are worth opening. A few simple habits make a big difference.

**Write like a person, not a brand.** Use "I" or "we," keep sentences short, and write the way you'd speak to a customer across a table. Automated emails don't have to feel automated.

**One email, one job.** Each message should have a single purpose: deliver a resource, share a tip, invite a reply, or present an offer. Multiple competing links usually mean fewer clicks overall.

**Make subject lines specific.** "Your checklist is inside" or "A quick question about your project" tends to work better than vague, clever lines. Tell people what they'll get.

**Ask for replies.** Inviting people to hit reply — "What's your biggest challenge with this?" — builds relationships, gives you valuable feedback, and can help deliverability.

**Use personalization carefully.** A first name in the greeting is fine. Personalizing based on what someone actually did — the guide they downloaded, the page they visited — is far more powerful.

**Review your sequences regularly.** Set a reminder to read through your automations every few months. Links break, offers change, and a quick refresh keeps everything accurate.

Good writing turns automation from "marketing machinery" into something your subscribers genuinely look forward to.

## Frequently asked questions

**How big does my list need to be before automation is worth it?**
Automation helps from the very first subscriber. A welcome series works just as well for 100 people as for 10,000 — and setting it up early means every new subscriber gets a great first impression.

**Can I switch platforms later without losing my list?**
Yes. Contacts can be exported and imported between platforms. What usually needs rebuilding is your automations, templates, and forms, so it's worth documenting your key sequences as you build them.

**Will my emails land in the inbox?**
Deliverability depends mostly on your sending practices: authenticate your domain, send to people who asked to hear from you, and remove inactive contacts. A good platform helps, but healthy list habits matter most.

**Do I need a CRM as well?**
If you sell services with a sales conversation, CRM features help you track deals and follow-ups. ActiveCampaign can cover both on suitable plans. If you sell products online with no sales step, you may not need a CRM at all.

**How many emails should a welcome series have?**
Three to five is a common, effective range. Focus on delivering value first, then introduce your offer naturally.
