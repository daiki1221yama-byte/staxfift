Every click you buy costs money, and every visitor who bounces off a generic homepage is money you'll never see again. When your job is to make paid campaigns profitable, the **best landing page builder for PPC** isn't the prettiest one. It's the one that lets you launch a matching page for every ad group fast, test it properly, and prove the results to a client.

This guide compares Unbounce, one of the best-known names in conversion-focused landing pages, with six alternatives: Leadpages, Instapage, Landingi, Swipe Pages, HubSpot landing pages, and Carrd. It's a research-based evaluation drawn from each vendor's official pricing and feature pages, help documentation, and recurring themes in public user reviews. I won't quote specific prices, because plans and amounts change and vary by billing cycle and region. Instead, I'll focus on how each tool charges, what it does best, and who it suits.

## The real pain: paid campaigns with a small team

If you're a PPC freelancer, an in-house performance marketer, or part of an agency with fewer than ten people, your week probably looks like this:

- **Too many campaigns, not enough pages.** Each ad group deserves a page that matches its message. Building them one by one in a CMS doesn't scale.
- **Developers are a bottleneck.** Waiting days for a page change means paying for clicks that land on the wrong message.
- **Testing is messy.** Running a clean A/B test, tracking conversions, and reading results shouldn't require three tools and a spreadsheet.
- **Clients want proof.** You need clear conversion data and a straightforward way to show what changed and why.
- **Costs scale in surprising ways.** Some builders charge by traffic, some by pages, some by domains or seats. A successful campaign can push you into a higher plan overnight.

A good PPC landing page builder solves these problems by putting building, testing, and reporting in one place, without needing a developer for every change.

## 5 criteria for choosing the best landing page builder for PPC

These criteria don't depend on any specific feature launch, so they stay useful as tools change.

### 1. Speed of building

Can you clone a page, change the headline and offer, and publish a new variant in minutes? Templates, reusable sections, and AI copy help all count here.

### 2. Testing and optimization

A/B testing is essential. Look for unlimited variants, clear statistics, and, ideally, automated optimization that sends each visitor to the version most likely to convert for them.

### 3. Message match

Dynamic text replacement swaps keywords on the page to match the search term or ad that brought the visitor. For search campaigns, this can make one page feel tailored to dozens of keywords.

### 4. Pricing that fits your traffic

Understand what the plan is capped by — visitors, pages, domains, or users — and what happens when you go over. Agencies should also check client sub-accounts and extra domains.

### 5. Integrations and tracking

You need your conversion pixels, analytics, CRM, and email tools to work cleanly. Custom scripts and a large integration library save hours.

### Unbounce, in plain English

Unbounce is a landing page builder made for marketers who live and breathe conversion rates. You build pages in a visual editor (with custom code if you want it), test as many variants as you like, and let AI route traffic to the best-performing version. It's long been a favorite of PPC specialists and agencies.

Here's what stands out from Unbounce's official pricing and feature pages:

- **Unlimited conversions on every plan.** Plans are capped by monthly traffic, users, and root domains, not by the number of leads you capture.
- **A builder for marketers.** Every plan includes the builder, AI copywriting, lead-gen forms, and hosting. From the Build plan, you can add custom scripts, pixels, code, and styling, and connect to a large integration library.
- **Serious testing.** The Experiment plan unlocks unlimited A/B testing, unlimited variants, manual traffic allocation, confidence intervals, and conversion reporting.
- **Dynamic text replacement.** Also from the Experiment plan, so pages can match the keywords in your ads.
- **Smart Traffic.** On the Optimize plan, Unbounce's AI traffic routing sends each visitor to the variant most likely to convert for people with similar attributes, such as device, location, and browser. Unbounce says it can start routing after as few as 50 visits.
- **Agency and managed options.** An Agency plan and a Concierge plan add higher limits, implementation services, and a dedicated customer success manager.
- **Flexible overages.** The FAQ says that if you go over your limits, your pages keep running and overage is charged as a one-time amount based on extra usage.

There's no permanent free plan, but there is a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. The FAQ notes some limits during the trial, such as a single user, no custom domains, and some traffic restrictions.

**The minor negative point:** the features that make Unbounce special — A/B testing, dynamic text replacement, and Smart Traffic — aren't on the entry plans. A/B testing and dynamic text replacement start on Experiment, and Smart Traffic is on Optimize. The entry plan also limits you to a handful of pages. For a PPC specialist, that means budgeting for a mid-level plan or higher from the start. And because plans are capped by traffic, a big campaign month can mean overage charges.

### Leadpages

Leadpages is a simpler builder aimed at small businesses, coaches, and solopreneurs. Its pricing page advertises unlimited visitors on every plan, with pages, sites, custom domains, and seats growing by tier. A/B testing and dynamic text replacement start on the Grow plan, and the Optimize plan adds AI traffic routing, auto-personalization, and heatmaps. There's a 7-day trial on paid plans; the pricing page says a card is required.

Leadpages is appealing when traffic is unpredictable and you want costs to stay flat. For deep experimentation across many clients, Unbounce's testing tools feel more central to the product.

### Instapage

Instapage is built for ad teams that collaborate on pages. It offers real-time visual collaboration, server-side A/B testing, dynamic text replacement on mid tiers, and ad-to-page personalization on its top tier. Plans are based on unique monthly visitors with unlimited pages and conversions, and it includes popups, sticky bars, a form builder, and AI content tools. There's a 14-day free trial on self-serve plans, but the pricing page says a credit card is required.

Instapage is a strong alternative if collaboration and personalization at scale are your priorities. Its advanced personalization sits on the custom top tier.

### Landingi

Landingi packs a lot into each tier: a form builder, popups, an AI page generator, server-side A/B testing from mid tiers, and over 400 templates. Plans are based on active landing pages and monthly visits, and the higher tier adds client sub-accounts, agency settings, and branding. There's a 14-day free trial for every plan; the pricing page says a card is sometimes requested and that trial visits are limited.

Landingi is a good fit for agencies that want sub-accounts and plenty of features without moving to custom pricing.

### Swipe Pages

Swipe Pages focuses on fast, mobile-friendly pages. Plans are capped by monthly visitors with unlimited conversions, and extra traffic is billed in blocks. Server-side A/B testing, personalization, and dynamic text replacement start on the Marketer plan, and client sub-accounts are included on Marketer and Agency plans. It also includes multi-step forms, funnels, and checkout with Stripe, PayPal, and Razorpay on every plan, plus AI credits for its builder. There's a 14-day free trial with no credit card required.

Swipe Pages is an attractive option for small agencies that want sub-accounts and testing at a lower tier.

### HubSpot landing pages

HubSpot offers a free landing page builder and form builder among its free tools, and its free plan supports up to two users with no credit card required. Paid Marketing Hub tiers are priced by marketing contacts, with an onboarding fee on Professional and Enterprise. A/B testing is listed in Marketing Hub, and the big advantage is that every page connects directly to HubSpot's CRM and email tools.

HubSpot makes sense when a client already runs on HubSpot. As a standalone PPC page builder, it's less specialized than Unbounce.

### Carrd

Carrd is a simple, inexpensive builder for one-page sites. Pro plans are billed yearly and priced by number of sites, with forms, custom domains, and payment embeds on higher Pro tiers. You can try Pro free for 7 days without a credit card. Carrd doesn't list A/B testing or dynamic text replacement, so it's better for quick, low-budget pages than for serious PPC optimization.

## Unbounce vs Instapage: the head-to-head PPC teams ask about

These two are often compared by performance marketers, so here's a closer look.

**Pricing logic.** Both cap plans by traffic. Unbounce also caps users and root domains and lists unlimited conversions. Instapage lists unlimited pages, conversions, and contacts with visitor-based tiers.

**Testing.** Both offer A/B testing on mid tiers. Unbounce adds Smart Traffic on its Optimize plan, which automatically routes visitors to the best variant. Instapage focuses on server-side testing and personalization.

**Collaboration.** Instapage highlights real-time visual collaboration and includes multiple team members on every tier. Unbounce adds users as you move up its plans.

**Trial.** Unbounce's 14-day trial requires no credit card. Instapage's 14-day trial requires one.

**The rule of thumb:** if you want strong testing plus automated AI traffic routing, and you like starting a trial without a card, begin with Unbounce. If team collaboration and ad-level personalization are your top priorities, Instapage is worth a demo.

**Pick: Unbounce.**

If you run search and social campaigns and need unlimited variants, dynamic text replacement, and AI traffic routing, Unbounce is built for exactly that workflow. Choose at least the Experiment plan so testing is included, and consider Optimize if you want Smart Traffic.

**Pick: Leadpages or Carrd.**

If you'd rather not think about traffic caps, Leadpages' unlimited-visitor plans keep costs predictable. Carrd is a good fallback for very simple, low-budget pages.

## How to get the most out of a 14-day free trial

Two weeks is enough to know whether a builder fits your PPC workflow.

1. **Days 1–2: Rebuild a real page.** Take a landing page from a live campaign and rebuild it from a template. Note how long it takes.
2. **Days 3–4: Connect tracking.** Add your conversion pixels and analytics, and connect your CRM or email tool. Submit a test lead.
3. **Days 5–6: Create variants.** Make two or three versions with different headlines or offers. Try dynamic text replacement with a few keywords.
4. **Days 7–11: Send a small slice of traffic.** Point a low-budget ad group at the new page. Watch conversions and how clearly results are reported.
5. **Days 12–14: Decide on the plan.** Match your expected traffic, domains, and users to the right tier, including any overage risk.

Remember that the Unbounce trial excludes custom domains, so plan to test on the default domain.

## 5 mistakes PPC marketers make when choosing a landing page builder

**1. Choosing the entry plan and missing testing.** Many builders put A/B testing on mid tiers. Check exactly where testing starts.

**2. Ignoring traffic caps.** A winning campaign can push you past your plan's limit. Understand overage terms before you scale.

**3. Forgetting message match.** Sending every ad to the same page wastes budget. Use dynamic text replacement or separate pages per ad group.

**4. Calling tests too early.** A few dozen visits aren't enough. Use confidence intervals or automated routing to avoid false winners.

**5. Underestimating client management.** Agencies need sub-accounts, extra domains, and clean reporting. Check these before you onboard clients.
