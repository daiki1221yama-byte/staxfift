Choosing a web host feels technical, but for most first websites it comes down to three questions: **what you're building, how much traffic you expect, and how much you want to manage yourself.** This guide walks through each so you can choose with confidence.

## The four main types of hosting

| Type | Best for | Typical skill needed | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Website builder | Simple business sites, portfolios | None | Less flexible, harder to move later |
| Shared hosting | Blogs and small sites on WordPress | Low | Shares resources with other sites |
| Managed WordPress | Growing blogs, content sites | Low | Costs more than shared |
| VPS / cloud | Developers, custom apps, high traffic | Medium–high | You handle more of the setup |

There's also a fifth option many people overlook: **static hosting** (services that serve pre-built HTML files). It's fast, secure and often free — great if you're comfortable with a little technical setup.

## Five things that actually matter

### 1. The renewal price, not the intro price

Many hosts advertise a low first-term price that rises sharply on renewal. Always check the **renewal rate** and the length of term the promo price requires. A slightly higher intro price with a fair renewal is often cheaper over three years.

### 2. Speed and location

Pick a host with servers (or a CDN) close to your audience. If your readers are in the US and Europe, make sure the host serves both regions well. Page speed affects both user experience and search rankings.

### 3. Free SSL and backups

An SSL certificate (the padlock in the browser) should be included free. Automatic daily backups with easy one-click restore will save you one day — check whether restores cost extra.

### 4. Support you can actually reach

Look for 24/7 live chat or ticket support, and read recent user feedback about response quality, not just speed.

### 5. An easy exit

Check whether you can export your site and whether migration help is offered. Avoid being locked in.

## Questions to ask before you buy

- What will I pay **after** the first term?
- Is there a money-back guarantee, and for how many days?
- Are domain registration, email and backups included or extra?
- Are there limits on storage, visits or "inodes" (number of files)?

> **Quick tip:** Register your domain separately from your hosting (for example, with a registrar that sells at cost). That way, switching hosts later never puts your domain at risk.

## Our bottom line

For a first blog or affiliate site, **shared or managed WordPress hosting** is usually the sweet spot: affordable, beginner-friendly and easy to grow. Choose a website builder only if you want zero technical work, and a VPS only if you know you need one.
