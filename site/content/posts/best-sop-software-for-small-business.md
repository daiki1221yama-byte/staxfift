## The real pain: your business lives in a few people's heads

If you own a business or lead operations or HR at a company with somewhere between five and a hundred people, you've probably felt at least a few of these:

- **Onboarding takes forever.** Each new hire shadows someone for weeks, and what they learn depends on who they shadow.
- **The same questions come up again and again.** "How do I issue a refund?" "Where's the login for the scheduling tool?" Every interruption pulls an experienced person away from their work.
- **Docs exist, but nobody reads them.** Processes are scattered across Google Docs, shared drives, and old emails. Some are out of date, and nobody knows which.
- **You can't tell who has learned what.** Did the new technician actually read the safety policy? Did the new account manager finish the client handover steps? Without tracking, you're guessing.
- **Roles are fuzzy.** When responsibilities aren't written down, tasks fall through the cracks or get done twice.
- **Documentation feels like a full-time job.** Writing step-by-step guides from scratch is slow, so it keeps getting pushed to "next week."

The right tool doesn't just store documents. It helps you create them faster, assign them to the right people, and confirm they've been read and understood.

## How to choose the best SOP software for small business: 5 criteria that last

These criteria focus on outcomes rather than features of the moment, so they stay useful as products change.

### 1. How fast you can create SOPs

Look at the editor, templates, screen recording, step capture, and AI drafting. The faster you can turn "how we do it" into a clear guide, the more likely documentation actually gets written.

### 2. Training and accountability

A wiki stores information. A training tool makes sure people learn it. Check for assignments, quizzes, completion tracking, and e-signatures for important policies.

### 3. Roles and structure

Can you organize content by role, department, or location, so a new hire sees exactly what applies to them? A clear map of who owns what is just as valuable as the processes themselves.

### 4. Findability

When someone has a question at 4 p.m. on a busy day, can they find the answer in seconds? Good search, including AI-powered answers, matters more than perfect folder structures.

### 5. Pricing model at your size

Some tools charge per user, some per content creator with free readers, and some quote based on team size. A ten-person team and an eighty-person team will get very different value from the same model.

### Notion

Notion is a flexible workspace where you can build docs, wikis, and databases. Paid plans are priced per member, guests are free, and there's a permanent free plan for individuals. The Business plan adds granular database permissions, private teamspaces, and verified badges that show which pages are up to date. Notion's AI can chat, generate, and autofill content.

Notion's pricing page doesn't describe built-in quizzes or training completion tracking. It suits teams that want one flexible space for everything and are happy to design their own SOP structure.

### Guru

Guru focuses on company knowledge that stays accurate. Its pricing page says it's tailored to your organization's scale and needs rather than sold per seat. Guru highlights AI search with cited, permission-aware answers, verification workflows, and integrations with more than a hundred tools, including Slack, Microsoft Teams, and Salesforce.

Guru's pricing page doesn't describe a self-serve trial; it suggests starting with a conversation. It fits larger teams that care most about answering questions accurately inside the tools they already use.

### Scribe

Scribe takes a capture-first approach. Using browser extensions and a desktop app, it records a process as you do it and turns it into a step-by-step guide with screenshots you can edit and redact. Pro plans are priced per seat, with Pro Team starting at five seats, and there's a free Basic tier. Enterprise adds role controls and automatic redaction of personal information.

Scribe's pricing page doesn't describe quizzes or training tracking. It's a strong fit if your main problem is the time it takes to write software walkthroughs.

### Whale

Whale is one of the closest alternatives to Trainual in purpose. It combines an AI SOP writer, a screen recorder, video-to-guide tools, checklists, training flows, and AI-generated quizzes. Plans are flat tiers that include a set number of content creators, while everyone else joins as a member with no per-seat fee, though member counts are limited below Enterprise. There's a free-forever plan with one creator and a small number of members.

Read assignments with due dates come with every plan, and e-signatures on completion start on the Advance plan. Whale suits small teams that want SOPs and training together and like the idea of paying mainly for creators.

### Document360

Document360 is knowledge base software with a strong publishing workflow. Its pricing page says pricing is customized around your configuration, including the number of editors, workspaces, languages, and security needs. It offers templates, approval workflows, roles and permissions, and AI writing and search. Screen capture and video recording are listed as add-ons in its AI suite.

Document360's pricing page doesn't describe quizzes or a self-serve trial. It fits teams that need a polished, well-governed knowledge base, especially one that may also serve customers.

### Slite

Slite is a team knowledge base with AI search and answers built in. Pricing is per user, and Slite offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. Features include doc ownership, doc verification workflows, user groups, permissions, an AI writing assistant, and Slack integration. Higher plans can search across connected tools.

Slite's pricing page doesn't describe quizzes or training completion tracking. It suits teams that want a clean, fast wiki where answers are easy to find.

Many small businesses start their SOPs in Notion and later wonder whether a dedicated tool like Trainual is worth it. Here's how they differ.

**Purpose.** Notion is a blank canvas for any kind of document or database. Trainual is built specifically for documenting processes, assigning them to people, and checking that they've been learned.

**Training features.** Trainual includes quizzes, e-signatures, assignments by role, and completion tracking. Notion's pricing page doesn't list built-in quizzes or training tracking, so you'd need to build workarounds or add other tools.

**The rule of thumb:** if you mainly need a shared place to write things down, Notion may be enough. If you need to onboard people consistently and know who has completed what, Trainual is built for that job.

## How to get the most out of a short free trial

Trainual's trial is short, so plan before you sign up. This approach works for most SOP tools.

1. **Before you start: pick one role.** Choose a role you hire for often, such as a customer support rep or field technician. List the ten processes that person needs in their first two weeks.
2. **Day 1: map roles and responsibilities.** Set up a simple structure for that role and its department. This makes the rest of the trial much easier.
3. **Days 2–3: create five SOPs.** Use templates, screen recording, or AI drafting. Time how long each one takes compared with writing it in a document.
4. **Day 4: add a quiz and a policy sign-off.** Create a short quiz for the most important process and require an e-signature on one key policy.
5. **Days 5–6: assign it to a real person.** Ask a recent hire or a colleague to go through the path and tell you where they got stuck.
6. **Day 7: check tracking and calculate cost.** Review completion data, then ask for pricing based on your actual team size and compare it with the time you'd save.

By the end, you'll know whether the tool will actually change how you onboard, not just where you store documents.

## 5 mistakes businesses make with SOP software

**1. Trying to document everything at once.** Start with the processes new hires need first. A small, used library beats a huge, ignored one.

**2. Writing SOPs nobody owns.** Every process needs an owner who keeps it up to date. Otherwise, it slowly drifts out of date.

**3. Skipping accountability.** If you don't assign content and check completion, people won't read it. Use assignments, quizzes, and sign-offs for what truly matters.

**4. Making SOPs too long.** Short steps, screenshots, and quick videos are easier to follow than long paragraphs.

**5. Choosing on price alone.** The cheapest tool isn't a bargain if your team won't use it. Think about the hours saved in onboarding and repeated questions.
