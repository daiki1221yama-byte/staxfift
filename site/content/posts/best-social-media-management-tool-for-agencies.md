## The real pain: too many clients, too few hands

If you run a small agency or work as a freelance social media manager handling several brands, you probably recognize most of these problems:

- **Account sprawl.** Every client has Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and maybe TikTok, Pinterest, or Google Business. Ten clients can quickly mean fifty or more profiles.
- **Approval bottlenecks.** Posts sit in a shared doc or a Slack thread waiting for a client to say "looks good." Meanwhile, the posting date slips.
- **Messy reporting.** Clients want proof that social media is working. Building a report by hand for each client every month eats hours you can't bill.
- **Missed messages.** Comments and DMs are spread across platforms and accounts. A slow reply to an unhappy customer reflects badly on your client and on you.
- **Seat costs that punish growth.** Some tools charge for every team member. Hiring one more freelancer or giving a client login access can push your costs up fast.
- **Login headaches.** Asking clients for their social media passwords is awkward and risky.

A good agency tool tackles as many of these as possible, so your team spends its time on content and strategy instead of admin.

## How to choose the best social media management tool for agencies: 5 criteria that last

These criteria don't depend on any single feature launch, so they stay useful even as products evolve.

### 1. Pricing model at agency scale

This matters more than anything else for agencies. Tools usually charge in one of three ways: per user (seat), per social account or channel, or by flat tiers that bundle a set number of accounts and users. A two-person agency with forty profiles and a ten-person agency with forty profiles will get very different value from the same plan. Do the math for your setup, not for a hypothetical team.

### 2. Client management and white-label reporting

Can you separate clients cleanly, give them their own view, and send reports with your agency's branding instead of the tool's? White-label or branded reports make your agency look polished and keep the client relationship with you.

### 3. Approval workflows

Look for both internal approvals (a junior writer's post checked by a manager) and client approvals (the client signs off before anything goes live). Check whether clients need a paid seat to approve posts.

### 4. Unified inbox and engagement

A single inbox for comments and messages across all clients' accounts saves time and prevents missed replies. Check which networks the inbox supports and which plan includes it.

### 5. Scheduling efficiency

Bulk scheduling, CSV uploads, content queues, and AI caption help all reduce the time it takes to fill a month's calendar for each client.

Map out which features you truly need before choosing a tier.

### Hootsuite

Hootsuite is one of the best-known names in social media management. Its plans (Standard, Professional, Advanced, and Enterprise) are sold per user, and the FAQ says this directly. Standard includes 10 social accounts, while higher plans offer unlimited accounts. A unified inbox is available on all plans, with automated replies and message routing added on higher tiers. Content review and approval is listed on Advanced, and advanced reporting lets you add your own logo or imagery to reports.

Hootsuite offers a 14-day free trial on its paid plans, but the trial has daily posting limits and doesn't include bulk scheduling. Its strengths are depth and analytics. For a small agency, the per-user model means costs grow with every team member you add.

### Buffer

Buffer keeps things simple. You pay per connected channel rather than per user or per client, and the pricing page says channels beyond ten cost less each. There's a free-forever plan with three channels, plus Essentials and Team plans with 14-day free trials. A community inbox is included on all plans, and an AI assistant is available across plans.

The Team plan is where agency features appear: unlimited users, approval workflows, custom access and permissions, and branded reports with your logo and a cover page. Buffer suits small agencies that value a clean, easy interface and don't need heavy reporting.

### Sprout Social

Sprout Social is a premium platform with deep analytics and customer care tools. Pricing is per seat, with plans ranging from Essentials through Enterprise. Entry plans include five social profiles, while Professional and Advanced offer unlimited profiles. A consolidated inbox comes in from Standard, and sentiment analysis in its Smart Inbox appears on Advanced. Presentation-ready, shareable reports are available through a Premium Analytics add-on.

Sprout Social offers a 30-day free trial with no credit card required. It's a strong choice for agencies that sell social care and in-depth reporting, but the per-seat model can be heavy for a small team.

### Agorapulse

Agorapulse is often praised for its inbox and reporting. Pricing is per user, and each plan includes 10 social profiles, with extra profiles sold as add-ons. White-label reporting and an all-in-one social inbox are on all plans, which is generous. Simple approval workflows for teammates or clients start on Professional, and the Advanced plan adds a Profile Connector that lets clients connect their own profiles securely, plus bulk scheduling of up to 200 posts by CSV.

Agorapulse offers a 30-day free trial with no credit card required. It suits agencies that handle a lot of community management and want strong inbox tools.

### Sendible

Sendible is built with agencies in mind. Its plans (Core, Plus, Premium, Elite, and Enterprise) are tiered by the number of client workspaces, each including a set number of profiles, and the pricing page promises unlimited users on all plans with no per-seat fees. Client Connect lets clients connect their profiles without sharing login details, and client dashboards let them preview and approve content. Approvals start on Plus, custom branded reports start on Premium, and full white label is a paid add-on.

Sendible offers a 14-day free trial with no card required. Its unlimited-user model is attractive if you work with many freelancers or want clients to log in.

### Later

Later started as a visual planner for Instagram and has grown into a broader scheduling tool. Plans are tiered by "social sets," where one set covers one profile on each supported platform, plus a set number of users. Content approval workflows, an external comment workflow for stakeholders without a Later account, and a social inbox are available on Growth and Scale, but not on Starter. Access groups help separate clients with their own media libraries and calendars.

Later offers a 14-day free trial on each plan. It suits agencies focused on visual platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and Pinterest.

## SocialPilot vs Hootsuite: the comparison many agencies make

When small agencies compare tools, SocialPilot and Hootsuite often end up on the same shortlist, so it's worth a closer look.

**Pricing philosophy.** Hootsuite charges per user. SocialPilot uses flat tiers based on social accounts, with a set number of users included and unlimited users on its top self-serve plan. For a small team managing many accounts, SocialPilot's model is usually easier to predict.

**Client features.** SocialPilot bundles client management, client seats, client approvals, and white-label reports into its Premium tier. Hootsuite lists team review and approval on its Advanced plan and branded reporting through its advanced reporting, but its pricing page doesn't describe a dedicated client portal.

**Trial experience.** Both offer 14-day trials. SocialPilot's doesn't need a credit card, according to its pricing page. Hootsuite's trial limits daily posts and doesn't include bulk scheduling, so it may not show you the full workflow.

**Depth.** Hootsuite offers broad analytics, competitor benchmarking, and an AI teammate across plans. If your agency sells advanced social listening or enterprise-style reporting, it may justify the higher per-user cost.

**The rule of thumb:** if you're a lean agency managing lots of client accounts and want client-facing features without per-seat costs, start with SocialPilot. If your clients expect enterprise-grade analytics and you have the budget for per-user pricing, Hootsuite is worth trialing.

## How to get the most out of a 14-day free trial

Two weeks goes by quickly. Here's a simple plan that works for most agency tools.

1. **Days 1–2: Connect one real client.** Pick a client with several networks and connect every profile. Note how smoothly the connection process works, especially if you can invite the client to connect profiles themselves.
2. **Days 3–5: Build two weeks of content.** Use bulk scheduling or a CSV upload if available. Try the AI caption tools on a few posts and see whether they save time or create more editing work.
3. **Days 6–8: Test approvals.** Send a batch of posts through your internal approval flow, then to the client. Watch how easy it is for the client to approve or comment.
4. **Days 9–11: Work from the inbox.** Reply to comments and messages from the unified inbox for a few days. Check whether every network you care about is supported.
5. **Days 12–14: Send a report and calculate cost.** Generate a branded report for the client. Then estimate accounts, users, and add-ons for all your clients and compare the real cost with what you pay today.

By the end, you'll know whether the tool saves your team hours each week or just moves the work around.

## 5 mistakes agencies make with social media tools

**1. Choosing on price per month instead of price per client.** A cheap plan that forces add-ons for every extra account or user can end up costing more. Calculate your cost per client.

**2. Sharing passwords with the team.** Use tools that let clients connect profiles securely and give team members their own logins with the right permissions.

**3. Skipping client approvals.** Posting without a clear sign-off process invites mistakes and awkward conversations. Build approvals into your workflow from day one.

**4. Treating reports as an afterthought.** Reports are what clients see. A clean, branded report that shows progress makes renewals easier.

**5. Ignoring the inbox.** Scheduling is only half the job. Missed comments and messages hurt your client's reputation and, eventually, yours.
