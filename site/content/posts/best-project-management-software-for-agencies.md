## The real pain: client work with thin margins

If you manage projects at a marketing agency, a creative studio, or a consultancy, your week probably includes some of these headaches:

- **Time tracking lives somewhere else.** People log hours in a separate app, a spreadsheet, or not at all, so you only find out a project went over budget after it's finished.
- **Retainers quietly leak.** Small extra requests pile up, and nobody notices that a monthly retainer has been running at a loss for three months.
- **You can't see who's overloaded.** One designer has twelve tasks due on Friday while another is waiting for work, and you don't find out until a deadline slips.
- **Invoicing takes hours.** At the end of the month, someone pulls time entries from one tool and builds invoices in another.
- **Clients want visibility.** They ask for status updates by email, and your team spends time writing reports instead of doing the work.
- **Tool sprawl.** A task app, a time tracker, a resource spreadsheet, and an invoicing tool all cost money and never quite agree with each other.

A good agency project management tool pulls as many of these jobs into one place as possible, so you can answer "Are we on track, and are we making money?" without a spreadsheet marathon.

## How to choose the best project management software for agencies: 5 criteria that last

These criteria don't depend on any single feature launch, so they stay useful even as products change.

### 1. Time tracking and billable hours

Can your team log time against tasks, mark it billable or non-billable, and report on it without extra tools? For agencies, time data is the foundation of everything else: budgets, invoicing, and profitability.

### 2. Budgets and profitability

Look for project budgets, retainer tracking, and reports that compare planned and actual hours or revenue. Many tools only offer this on higher tiers or as add-ons, so check carefully.

### 3. Resource and workload management

You need to see who's available, who's overloaded, and whether you can take on a new client next month. Workload views and capacity planning turn guesswork into real decisions.

### 4. Client collaboration

Can you invite clients to see progress, approve work, or comment, without giving them access to everything? Check how guests or client users are handled and whether they count as paid seats.

### 5. Total cost at your team size

Work out the full cost for your real headcount, including minimum seat counts, add-ons, and the extra tools you could cancel. A tool that includes time tracking and invoicing might replace two other subscriptions.

### Teamwork.com, in plain English

Teamwork.com is a project management platform designed specifically for agencies and other client-services businesses. Instead of treating time tracking, budgets, and invoicing as add-ons, it builds them into its plans, so tasks, hours, and money all live in the same place.

Here's what stands out from Teamwork.com's official pricing page and homepage:

- **A real free plan.** The Free plan supports up to five users and includes logging time plus task, list, board, Gantt, and table views.
- **Billable time on the entry paid tier.** The Basics plan adds billable time tracking and reporting, along with unlimited project templates.
- **Invoicing and retainers.** The Accelerate plan adds client invoicing from logged time, time budgets and retainers, and workload and live capacity planning. It also lists integrations with HubSpot and QuickBooks.
- **Profitability and resource planning.** The Optimize plan adds multi-currency financial budgets, revenue, cost, and profitability insights, tentative and active resource planning, and scheduling by role or skill. Salesforce and NetSuite integrations are listed here too.
- **Lots of connections.** The homepage cites more than 150 integrations.
- **A separate help desk.** Teamwork Desk, a help desk product, is priced separately for teams that also handle client support tickets.

Paid plans are priced per user and billed annually, and some plans have minimum seat counts. Teamwork.com offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required.

**The minor negative point:** Teamwork.com saves its most agency-specific features for its middle and upper tiers. Invoicing from logged time and retainer budgets start on Accelerate, and profitability insights start on Optimize, which is a "contact sales" plan. Paid plans also have minimum seat counts. If you're a very small studio that mainly needs a task board, you may pay for more structure than you need. Map the features you'll actually use to a plan before you commit.

### Asana

Asana is one of the most widely used work management tools, and it's flexible enough for almost any team. There's a free Personal plan for up to two users, then paid plans priced per user. Timeline and Gantt views, reporting dashboards, custom templates, and unlimited free guests start on Starter. Advanced adds a comparison of estimated and actual time and resource management. Billable hours and project budgets are handled through a Timesheets and Budgets add-on.

Asana is a great fit if your agency values clean, flexible task management and is happy to add timesheets and budgets as needed. Teams that bill heavily by the hour may prefer a tool where those features are part of the core plan.

### monday.com

monday.com is known for colorful, highly customizable boards. Its work management product has a free plan for up to two seats, then paid plans priced per seat. Timeline and Gantt views, guest access, and automations start on Standard. Time tracking is on Pro, and resource and portfolio management are on Enterprise. monday.com also sells a separate CRM product that includes quotes and invoices.

The pricing page says you can start free with no credit card needed, and a trial of the Pro plan is available. monday.com suits agencies that want to design their own workflows and enjoy a visual tool, though core agency finance features like budgets aren't clearly listed on the work management plans.

### ClickUp

ClickUp tries to be an all-in-one workspace, covering tasks, docs, goals, and more. It has a Free Forever plan, then paid tiers priced per user. Guest permissions and unlimited Gantt charts come with Core. Timesheets, resource management, workload views, and advanced dashboards come with Business. ClickUp lists templates and more than 50 native integrations on all tiers.

ClickUp is a strong choice for agencies that want maximum features for the money and don't mind a learning curve. Billable-hours reporting, invoicing, and budgets aren't listed on the pricing page, so you may still need a separate tool for finance.

### Wrike

Wrike is a structured work management platform that scales from small teams to large organizations. It has a free plan, a Team plan for two to fifteen users, and a Business plan for five to two hundred users. Interactive Gantt charts and shareable dashboards start on Team, and templates come with Business. Time tracking, budgeting with billable hours, advanced resource and capacity planning, and advanced reporting are on the Pinnacle plan.

Wrike offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. It suits larger agencies that need serious planning and reporting, but the financial features sit on its higher tiers.

### Smartsheet

Smartsheet feels like a spreadsheet that grew into a project management tool, which makes it comfortable for teams that think in rows and columns. Plans are priced per member. Gantt view and templates are on all plans. Business adds timeline view, workload tracking, unlimited automations, and unlimited free guests. Time and expense tracking come through a Resource Management add-on.

Smartsheet offers a free trial. It's a good fit for operations-minded agencies that love spreadsheets, though agency-specific billing features aren't part of the core plans.

### Basecamp

Basecamp takes a different approach. Instead of charging per user, it offers flat-priced packages, with the top package covering unlimited users and projects. Every package includes core tools like message boards, to-dos, scheduling, and automatic check-ins, and time tracking is included on most paid packages. Basecamp offers a free account with one project and a 30-day free trial on paid packages, with no credit card required.

Basecamp is great for agencies that want calm, simple communication with clients and teammates. It doesn't list budgets, invoicing, Gantt charts, or resource management, so it's not built for detailed financial tracking.

## Teamwork.com vs Asana: the comparison most agencies make

For many agencies, the shortlist comes down to Teamwork.com and Asana, so it's worth a closer look.

**Philosophy.** Asana is a general-purpose work management tool used by marketing, product, operations, and many other teams. Teamwork.com is built specifically for client services, so its plans revolve around time, budgets, and billing.

**Time and money.** Teamwork.com includes billable time tracking from Basics, then invoicing, retainers, and budgets on Accelerate. Asana offers estimated vs. actual time on Advanced and handles billable hours and budgets through an add-on.

**Resource planning.** Both offer workload and resource tools on their mid-level and higher plans.

**Free plans.** Teamwork.com's free plan supports up to five users, while Asana's free Personal plan supports two.

**The rule of thumb:** if your agency bills by the hour or manages retainers, start with Teamwork.com, because its plans are built around those workflows. If your work is mostly campaign or content production and billing happens elsewhere, Asana's flexibility may suit you better.

## How to get the most out of a 14-day free trial

Two weeks goes fast. Here's a simple plan to make it count.

1. **Days 1–2: Set up one real client project.** Pick an active project rather than a demo. Recreate its tasks, deadlines, and team members using a template if possible.
2. **Days 3–5: Have everyone log time.** Ask the team to track every hour on that project, including non-billable work. Notice how easy or annoying it is to log time each day.
3. **Days 6–8: Set a budget.** Add a time or money budget to the project and watch how actual hours compare with the plan. If you run retainers, set one up.
4. **Days 9–11: Check workload.** Look at the workload or capacity view. Can you see who's overloaded? Try moving a task and see how the view updates.
5. **Days 12–14: Run a mock invoice and calculate real cost.** Turn logged time into a draft invoice or report. Then estimate seats, plan level, and any tools you could cancel, and compare with your current setup.

By the end, you'll know whether the tool shows you the health of your projects or just adds another login.

## 5 mistakes agencies make when choosing project management software

**1. Choosing for the project manager instead of the whole team.** If designers and writers find time tracking painful, they won't log hours and your reports will be wrong. Let the people doing the work test the tool.

**2. Ignoring finance features.** A beautiful task board doesn't tell you whether a retainer is profitable. Check how time, budgets, and invoicing work before you decide.

**3. Forgetting seat minimums and add-ons.** Some plans require a minimum number of users, and key features may be add-ons. Calculate the full cost for your real team.

**4. Giving clients too much access.** Client visibility is great, but it should be controlled. Check how guests and client users work and what they can see.

**5. Migrating everything at once.** Move one or two projects first, get the team comfortable, then bring over the rest. A gradual switch reduces disruption.
